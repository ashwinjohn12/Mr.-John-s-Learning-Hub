Ten repairs

0 / 10 completeOne question at a time · two stages of four
1A student writes 3/8=0.38. What is the first repair?Stage 1 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Use the fraction bar as division.

Calculate 3÷8 far enough to see 0.375 before comparing.

2A student says 0.9<0.85 because 9<85.Stage 1 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Align both decimals to hundredths.

Rewrite 0.9 as 0.90, then compare tenths and hundredths.

3A student aligns 4.8+0.36 by the final digits.Stage 1 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Create columns headed ones, tenths, and hundredths.

Rewrite 4.8 as 4.80 before adding.

4A student says 1.2×0.5=6.Stage 1 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Use one-half as a multiplication benchmark.

Half of 1.2 must be less than 1.2.

5A student changes 6.3÷0.9 to 6.3÷9.Stage 2 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Make the divisor a whole number without changing the quotient.

Multiply both 6.3 and 0.9 by 10.

6A student evaluates 8−2.5+1.5 as 8−4.Stage 2 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Treat subtraction and addition as equal-priority operations.

Calculate 8−2.5 before adding 1.5.

7A student writes 7%=0.7.Stage 2 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Percent means out of 100.

Write 7% as 7÷100 before placing the decimal.

8A student finds 35% of 80 as 35×80.Stage 2 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Change the percent to a decimal multiplier.

Use 0.35×80, not 35×80.

9A student orders 0.6 before 0.59 as though 6<59.Stage 3 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Align both values to hundredths.

Rewrite 0.6 as 0.60 before comparing.

10A student reports 3.2×2.1=67.2.Stage 3 of 2

Choose an answer, then check it.

Estimate the product before placing the decimal.

Since 3.2×2.1 is near 6, use the placement close to 6.
