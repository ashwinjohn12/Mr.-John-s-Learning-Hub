Mean and Mode

START HERE

The big ideas

Mean is fair share; mode is greatest frequency. They can tell different, equally valid stories.

Mean redistributes the total

Add every value and divide by the number of observations; multiply back to verify the total.

Mode is about frequency

The mode is the most frequent value or category, not the greatest number.

📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

1. Mean as fair share

Mean = total ÷ number of observations. Estimate before calculating and multiply back to check.

Representation connection: Redistribute counters equally, then record the same process in a table and equation.

Common mix-up: Dividing by the largest value instead of the count.

2. Mode as most frequent

Count frequencies; datasets can have one mode, several tied modes, or no mode.

Representation connection: A dot plot or frequency table makes repetition visible.

Common mix-up: Calling the maximum the mode.

3. Compare measures

Mean uses every value while mode uses frequency, so they need not agree.

Representation connection: Place the numerical mean beside a frequency display.

Common mix-up: Assuming all “averages” must be equal.

4. Interpret in context

Attach the correct unit and describe the dataset summary without claiming every observation equals it.

Representation connection: Connect statistic → unit → factual context sentence.

Common mix-up: Turning a summary into an individual guarantee.

🧩

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Find mean of 6,8,8,10,13.
  1. Total 45.
  2. Five values.
  3. 45÷5=9.
  4. Check 9×5=45.

Conclusion: Mean 9.

Example 2 · Find the mode.
  1. 8 occurs twice; all others once.

Conclusion: Mode 8.

Example 3 · Can there be two modes?
  1. Yes: 2,2,5,5,8 has two values tied for greatest frequency.

Conclusion: Modes 2 and 5.

Example 4 · What does mean 9 points mean?
  1. It is fair-share centre of the total.
  2. It does not mean every score was 9.

Conclusion: A contextual summary, not a guarantee.
