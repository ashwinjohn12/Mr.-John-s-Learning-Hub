START HERE

The big ideas

An outlier is contextually unusual; its effect and treatment depend on the statistic and reporting purpose.

Outliers can pull statistics

Extreme values change the total and often move mean strongly; median may move much less.

Context decides reporting

Never delete automatically; decide from validity and purpose and disclose the choice.

📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

1. Identify unusual values

Look for separation from the distribution and check context.

Representation connection: Dot plots reveal gaps while tables preserve exact values.

Common mix-up: Calling every maximum an outlier.

2. Outliers move the mean

Calculate with and without the value to quantify its effect.

Representation connection: Side-by-side totals show why fair-share mean moves.

Common mix-up: Saying mean cannot change because most values stay.

3. Median and mode may resist

Central position and frequency can be less sensitive to a single extreme.

Representation connection: Ordered data expose stable middle positions.

Common mix-up: Assuming every average responds equally.

4. Include or exclude with reason

Use reporting purpose, validity, and transparency.

Representation connection: Evidence table: purpose → include/exclude → disclosed effect.

Common mix-up: Deleting silently.

🧩

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Outlier in 9,10,10,11,12,35?
  1. Most values cluster 9–12.
  2. 35 is separated.

Conclusion: 35 is a clear high outlier.

Example 2 · Mean effect?
  1. With: 87÷6=14.5.
  2. Without: 52÷5=10.4.

Conclusion: Mean rises 4.1.

Example 3 · Median effect?
  1. With: 10.5.
  2. Without: 10.

Conclusion: Much smaller change.

Example 4 · Should an outage value disappear?
  1. Depends on reporting purpose.
  2. Disclose the decision either way.

Conclusion: No automatic rule.
