START HERE

The big ideas

Every point on a circle is the same distance from its centre, so radius controls the whole circle.

One centre, one distance

A circle is the set of points equally distant from its centre.

Diameter crosses the centre

A diameter joins two circle points through the centre, so it contains two radii.

📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

1. Circle parts

The centre anchors the circle. A radius joins the centre to the circle; a diameter crosses the centre; circumference is the outside distance. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of calling any chord a diameter.

Model: On a radius-4 cm circle, every radius is 4 cm and every diameter is 8 cm.

Common mix-up: Calling any chord a diameter.

2. Radius and diameter

A diameter is made of two radii placed end to end: d=2r and r=d÷2. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of using d=r or doubling a diameter.

Model: r=6 cm gives d=12 cm; d=15 cm gives r=7.5 cm.

Common mix-up: Using d=r or doubling a diameter.

3. Central angles

Radii split a circle into central angles around one point. A full turn is 360°. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of using 180° for a full circle.

Model: Angles 90°, 120°, and 150° total 360°.

Common mix-up: Using 180° for a full circle.

4. Construct and verify

Set a compass opening equal to the radius, keep the point fixed, and turn through a full circle. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of measuring the diameter as the compass opening.

Model: A requested 5 cm radius must measure 5 cm in several directions.

Common mix-up: Measuring the diameter as the compass opening.

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Name parts · A segment runs from centre O to point A on the circle.
  1. Its first endpoint is the centre.
  2. Its second endpoint lies on the circle.
  3. Therefore it is a radius.
  4. Check that radius is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: radius

Relate measures · A circle has diameter 18 cm.
  1. Use r=d÷2.
  2. Calculate 18÷2.
  3. Check that two radii rebuild the diameter.
  4. Check that r=9 cm is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: r=9 cm

Central angle · Two angles are 115° and 95°. Find the third.
  1. A full turn is 360°.
  2. Subtract 115+95 from 360.
  3. Check all three total 360°.
  4. Check that 150° is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: 150°

Construction · Construct a circle with diameter 10 cm.
  1. Convert to radius: 10÷2=5.
  2. Open the compass 5 cm.
  3. Verify several centre-to-circle distances.
  4. Check that 5 cm compass opening is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: 5 cm compass opening
