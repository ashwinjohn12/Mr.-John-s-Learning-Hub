Choose an answer when ready.

START HERE

The big ideas

For every circle, circumference divided by diameter is the same constant: π.

π is a ratio

C÷d is about 3.14 for every circle.

Estimate before calculating

Circumference should be a little more than three diameters.

📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

1. Measure around and across

Measure circumference around the edge and diameter straight across the centre. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of measuring a noncentral chord as diameter.

Model: C≈31.4 cm and d=10 cm gives C÷d≈3.14.

Common mix-up: Measuring a noncentral chord as diameter.

2. Connect to π

π is the constant ratio C/d, not exactly 3.14. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of treating π as a unit or exact 3.14.

Model: C/d=π, so C=πd.

Common mix-up: Treating π as a unit or exact 3.14.

3. Choose a formula

Use C=πd when diameter is known or C=2πr when radius is known. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of using area formula πr².

Model: r=5 m gives C≈2π(5)=31.4 m.

Common mix-up: Using area formula πr².

4. Interpret and round

Circumference uses linear units; round only at the end to the requested precision. Use the displayed model to preserve every quantity. Estimate or compare before accepting a result; this exposes the common error of writing square metres or rounding midway.

Model: A 7.2 m radius track has C≈45.24 m to hundredths.

Common mix-up: Writing square metres or rounding midway.

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Diameter known · Find C for d=14 cm.
  1. Estimate a little over 42 cm.
  2. Calculate π×14.
  3. Round as requested and attach cm.
  4. Check that ≈43.98 cm is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: ≈43.98 cm

Radius known · Find C for r=3.5 m.
  1. Use C=2πr.
  2. Calculate 2π(3.5).
  3. Check against three diameters: 3×7=21.
  4. Check that ≈21.99 m is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: ≈21.99 m

Find diameter · C=31.4 cm. Find d.
  1. Rearrange d=C÷π.
  2. Calculate 31.4÷π.
  3. Check πd returns about 31.4.
  4. Check that ≈10.00 cm is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: ≈10.00 cm

Compare · Which needs more edging: d=8 m or r=5 m?
  1. Convert or calculate both circumferences.
  2. 8π≈25.13 and 10π≈31.42.
  3. Compare with the same unit.
  4. Check that the r=5 m circle is reasonable and uses the required unit or representation.

Conclusion: the r=5 m circle
