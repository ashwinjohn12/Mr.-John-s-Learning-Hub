02

FIRST 3D BUILD

First Code — Build to an Exact Size

No coding in this mission. In CAD, your first “instructions” are shapes and dimensions.

A model that only looks right may still print at the wrong size. CAD lets you design with exact measurements.

Basic Shapes → BoxSelect shape → dimension valuesRuler → optional helperUndo
WIDTH · 40 mmside to side LENGTH · 20 mmfront to back HEIGHT · 4 mmup and down

Orbit changes your view, not the object's dimensions. Width, length and height stay the same even when the model looks rotated on screen.

DRAG A HANDLE≈ approximateTYPE A NUMBER= exact

Dimensions are the measurements of your object. In this mission we use millimetres (mm).

Set the Box

Width 40 mm · Length 20 mm · Height 4 mm.

Change one thing

Change either width or length on purpose. Observe the result, then return to 40 × 20 × 4 mm.

MODEL LOOKS RIGHT BUT SIZE IS WRONG? Select the Box and read the actual dimension values. Do not judge size only by how large it looks on screen.

✓ Your Box measures 40 mm × 20 mm × 4 mm and sits flat on the Workplane.

COMBINE + CUT

Make It React — Align, Group & Hole

In CAD, shapes “react” to each other when you line them up, combine them, or subtract one from another.

JOIN SOLIDS

OVERLAP → ALIGN → SELECT BOTH → GROUP

CUT MATERIAL

SET SHAPE TO HOLE → OVERLAP → SELECT BOTH → GROUP → CUT

ALIGN line shapes up accuratelyGROUP applies the relationship between selected shapesSOLID + SOLID Group joinsSOLID + HOLE Group cuts

Group does different things depending on what you selected: Solid + Solid joins; Solid + Hole cuts.

Basic Shapes → BoxBasic Shapes → CylinderShape panel → Solid / HoleSelect 2+ shapes → AlignSelect 2+ shapes → GroupUngroup → repairUndo → repair
A — Align + Group

Add a second solid. Overlap it with the Box. Select both. Use Align. Confirm the solids overlap, then Group and orbit around the combined part.

B — Cut a Hole

Add a Cylinder. Change it to Hole. Make it pass completely through the practice solid. Select both, Group, then inspect from underneath.

YOUR 3D DESIGN

Build It — Maker Tag

Design a small personalized tag that could be attached to a backpack, key ring, zipper, equipment bin, or classroom tool.

Your goal is a simple, accurate, printable object — not the most complicated object you can make.

STUCK? TRY 4

1 CHECK — check what is selected before clicking another tool, then check dimensions, Solid vs Hole, overlap, and Workplane contact. 2 VIEW — orbit, zoom, and inspect top, side, and underside. 3 UNDO / RETRY — undo the last incorrect change and retry deliberately. 4 PARTNER — explain what you wanted the model to do and what happened instead.

Still stuck? Ask for help and say what you checked, viewed, and retried.

Do not export a broken design and hope the printer fixes it.

HOLE DISAPPEARED?

Check that the cutting shape was set to Hole before Group.

HOLE DID NOT CUT THROUGH?

Inspect from the side and make the Hole taller than the base.

GROUP DID NOTHING?

Confirm both shapes are selected.

PART LOOKS CONNECTED BUT ISN'T?

Orbit around it and confirm the solids overlap.

OBJECT IS FLOATING?

Return the required base to the Workplane.

05

OPTIONAL CHALLENGE

Level It Up

Only after your Maker Tag passes the required checklist. Choose one while you wait for your checkpoint.

⭐ Raised Name / Initials

Add short raised text or initials. Keep the lettering thick and readable. If Text is unavailable or slow, use a simple raised symbol or primitive-shape pattern instead.

⭐ Recessed Detail

Use Text or a simple shape as a Hole to create a recessed feature without cutting through the whole base.

⭐⭐ Border / Pattern

Use Duplicate and Align to create a small repeated geometric pattern. Keep it simple.

⭐⭐ Shape Remix

Replace the simple rectangular base with a custom outline made from 2–4 overlapping primitive shapes while staying inside the same size limits.

MISSION 6 LIMIT: no articulated models, gears, threads, complex hinges, downloaded meshes, giant prints, or multi-part assemblies.

When every Maker Tag requirement is complete and your printability check passes, add your name to Ready-for-Check.

06

SHOW WHAT YOU KNOW

Checkpoint

Bring your digital Tinkercad model when you are called. You do not need a finished physical print. The checkpoint is individual.

Check 1 — Inspect the Model

Orbit around it, show the base, show the through-hole, show exact dimensions, and confirm the model sits on the Workplane.

Check 2 — Explain the Design

Explain which primitive shapes you used, why Align was useful, what Group did with two solids, what Group did with Solid + Hole, why the model should be printable, and one personal design choice.

Check 3 — Change It

Make one teacher-chosen edit. Then PREDICT → CHANGE → ORBIT / CHECK → EXPLAIN. Restore a valid print-ready state before export.

After Your Check

MISSION COMPLETE · RETRY ONE SKILL · SUPPORT ROUTE

A printer being busy does not affect your checkpoint result. Mission 6 mastery is about your CAD design.
