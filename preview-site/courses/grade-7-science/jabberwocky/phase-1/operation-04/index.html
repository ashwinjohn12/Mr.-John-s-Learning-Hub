SCIENCE YOU WILL USE

A food web is a network, not a list.

Producer

An organism that captures energy—usually sunlight—to make its own food. Producers begin most feeding pathways.

Consumer

An organism that gets energy by eating producers or other consumers.

Decomposer

An organism such as a fungus or bacterium that obtains energy by breaking down dead material and wastes.

Food web

A model showing many connected feeding relationships. The arrow points from the food or energy source toward the organism receiving the energy.

Competition

Two organisms use the same limited resource, such as food, water, shelter, space, or light.

Symbiosis

A close relationship between species. It can include mutualism, commensalism, or parasitism.

Remember: energy flows through an ecosystem and is eventually released as heat. Matter such as water and nutrients can be cycled and reused. Do not draw an energy arrow from a decomposer back to a producer.

HANDS-ON INVESTIGATION // MODEL LAB

String Web Investigation

35–45 min

Before mapping an alien ecosystem, your team will build a physical Earth food web. The goal is to see how quickly one change can spread through a connected system.

Equipment per team

  • 8 printed ecosystem cards
  • Several short pieces of yarn or string
  • Small paper arrows, sticky-note arrows, or pencil arrows on the table
  • Operation 04 field sheet

Reusable setup: laminate one card set per team and keep short yarn pieces in envelopes for future classes.

Earth model cards

  • Sunlight — energy source
  • Prairie Grass — producer
  • Wildflower — producer
  • Grasshopper — consumer
  • Rabbit — consumer
  • Mouse — consumer
  • Hawk — consumer
  • Fungi & Bacteria — decomposers
How to arrange the model: lay all cards flat on the table. Use a separate piece of yarn for each connection. Place an arrow marker beside each yarn piece to show energy direction. Do not hold one giant string around the group.

Build the web

  1. Place the cards where everyone can reach them. Keep Sunlight to one side because it is an energy source, not an organism.
  2. Start with producers. Connect Sunlight to Prairie Grass and Wildflower.
  3. Add feeding connections using separate yarn pieces: Prairie Grass → Grasshopper, Rabbit, and Mouse; Wildflower → Grasshopper and Rabbit; Grasshopper → Mouse; Mouse → Hawk; Rabbit → Hawk.
  4. Show direction. Add an arrow marker so every feeding arrow points from the food toward the organism receiving energy.
  5. Add decomposers. Use a different placement or label to show that dead material and wastes from every organism can provide resources to Fungi & Bacteria.
  6. Trace three pathways from Sunlight to a consumer. Notice that one organism can belong to more than one pathway.

NETWORK STRESS TEST

Temporarily remove Prairie Grass.

Remove the Prairie Grass card and the yarn pieces touching it. Leave the rest of the web in place. Follow the remaining strings and predict what may happen.

Direct effects

Which organisms lose a food source immediately?

Indirect effects

Which organisms might be affected later even though they do not eat Prairie Grass?

Competition

Would any consumers depend more heavily on Wildflower? What could that change?

Model limit: removing a card does not prove exactly what would happen in a real ecosystem. It helps us make a testable prediction about possible effects.

Your teacher may already provide the packet. One physical model card set can be reused; one Food Web V1 sheet per team is enough.

MISSION CHECKPOINT // BEFORE PLANETARY ANALYSIS

Four rules your team must use.

1

Arrows show energy transfer: food → eater.

2

A food web can have many pathways. Do not force every organism into one straight chain.

3

Decomposers are part of the web. They obtain energy from dead material and wastes.

4

A prediction is not a certainty. Use words such as may, could, likely, and because when evidence is incomplete.
