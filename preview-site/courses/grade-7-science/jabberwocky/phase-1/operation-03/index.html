SCIENCE YOU WILL USE

Evidence first. Imagination second.

Adaptation

A structural or behavioural feature that helps an organism survive and meet its needs in a particular environment.

Structure & function

The shape or form of a body structure affects what that structure can do. A feature that works well in one situation may be less useful in another.

Niche

An organism’s way of living in its ecosystem—including where it lives, what resources it uses, when it is active, and how it interacts with its surroundings.

Scientific reconstruction

A model built from evidence. Features that are not supported by evidence must be labelled as uncertain rather than treated as fact.

Important: organisms do not simply choose a new adaptation because they need one. This operation asks whether a structure or behaviour fits an environment; it is not a simulation of how adaptations evolve.

HANDS-ON INVESTIGATION // MODEL LAB

Survival Structures Lab

30–40 min

Before JCEC lets your team infer adaptations from alien evidence, you must demonstrate that different structures perform differently under different conditions.

Equipment per team

  • 1 plastic spoon
  • 1 pair of tweezers
  • 1 clothespin or small tongs
  • 1 pair of chopsticks
  • 3 trays or resource zones
  • Craft objects such as pom-poms, beads/paper clips, and loops of pipe cleaner or rubber bands
  • 1 collection cup and timer

Teacher substitutions are fine. The important part is comparing several structures fairly across several different resource conditions.

Team jobs

  • Tester: uses each structure during the timed trial.
  • Timer: keeps every trial the same length.
  • Recorder: records the number of resources collected.
  • Quality Checker: resets the resource zone and watches for fair testing.

Your teacher may assign jobs or have your team divide them. Switch the Tester between resource zones so more than one person handles the equipment.

A
Open Ground

Large objects spread across an open tray.

B
Narrow Spaces

Small objects placed in cups, cells, or shallow crevices.

C
Tangled Cover

Looped or irregular objects mixed around simple barriers.

Procedure

  1. Set the same trial time for every test—20 seconds is recommended.
  2. Test all four structures in Resource Zone A. Move one resource at a time into the collection cup. Do not use your fingers to help.
  3. Record the number collected and reset the zone before the next structure.
  4. Repeat for Resource Zones B and C using the same rules and trial time.
  5. Compare your results. Identify which structure performed best in each condition and which structure was most versatile overall.
  6. Look for trade-offs. Decide whether any structure was best everywhere. Use your data—not preference—to support your answer.
Your teacher may already provide this sheet. One lab data sheet and one reconstruction sheet per team are enough.

LAB DEBRIEF

“Best” depends on the job and the environment.

STRUCTURE→FUNCTION→ENVIRONMENT→NICHE

A spoon may perform very well in an open tray but poorly in a narrow space. That does not make the spoon a “bad” structure. It means the usefulness of a structure depends on what the organism needs to do and where it lives.
