CLASS 1

Learn the Science

Start with four ideas.

🌿Producer

Makes its own food, usually using sunlight.

🐾Consumer

Gets energy by eating other organisms.

🍄Decomposer

Breaks down dead material and returns matter to the environment.

🕸️Food web

Shows many feeding connections in an ecosystem.

⚡ Energy flows.

Energy enters through producers and moves to consumers.

♻️ Matter cycles.

Water and carbon can be reused as they move through living and non-living parts of an ecosystem.

FOOD→EATER

The arrow points where the energy goes. Example: grass → rabbit.

Need help remembering the arrow direction?

Read the arrow as “gives energy to.” Grass gives energy to a rabbit when the rabbit eats it, so the arrow is grass → rabbit.

CLASS 3

Change the Web

TEAM WORK

Use the change for your continent. Cover or mark the organism or resource that changes, then follow the connected arrows.

What changed?

A dry spring causes Bluecrest Grass to decline sharply for three weeks.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

A storm removes many fruiting Glowfruit branches.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

Several days of unusually high waves scrape Tidefilm Algae from exposed rocks.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

A season produces very few Saltseed seeds.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

A late snowmelt shortens the growing season for Tundra Mat.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

Goldstem Grass is reduced in one large area after an unusually dry month.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

Bark Grub numbers drop suddenly in one forest patch.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.
What changed?

A temporary drop in surface-insect abundance occurs after several days of heavy rain.

If this changes, then__________may change because__________.

Choose one clear likely effect. You do not need to explain every possible change.
