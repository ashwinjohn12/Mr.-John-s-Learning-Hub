CLASS 1

Learn the Science

You only need four ideas for this mission.

🦶Structure

A body part or physical feature.

⚙️Function

What a structure does or helps an organism do.

🛡️Adaptation

A feature or behaviour that helps an organism survive.

💧Needs

Things organisms require, such as food, water, shelter, space and suitable conditions.

Need a quick example?

Structure: a duck's webbed feet.

Function: the feet push against water.

How it helps: they help the duck swim to find food and move through its habitat.

Learn more: What is a niche?

A niche is an organism's way of living in an ecosystem—where it lives, what it uses, and how it interacts with its surroundings. You do not need this word to complete Mission 2.

CLASS 1

Survival Structures Lab

TEAM WORK

Question: Which structure works best in which environment?

Structuresspoon · tweezers · clothespin/tongs · chopsticks
Resource zonesopen ground · narrow spaces · tangled cover
  1. Set up the three resource zones your teacher gives you.
  2. Use one structure for the same amount of time in each trial.
  3. Count how many resources you can move to the collection cup.
  4. Repeat with every structure using the same time, resources and rules.
  5. Compare your results. Decide which structure worked best in each environment.
Keep it fair.Change the structure. Keep the time, resource amount and rules the same.

Gyre · Frumious Basin

Ridgeback Grazer

stream valley below a wind-exposed ridge

1
TRACK SURVEY

Broad four-toed tracks cross loose valley soil without sinking deeply. The same trail narrows toward rocky higher ground.

2
FEEDING TRACE

Low plants beside the stream show wide cropped edges. Hard stems are crushed rather than torn into narrow strips.

3
THERMAL IMAGE

A large body-shaped heat signal remains warm even when the surrounding air drops near freezing.

4
FIELD CAMERA

The organism lowers its body behind boulders during strong ridge winds and becomes active again when winds weaken.
