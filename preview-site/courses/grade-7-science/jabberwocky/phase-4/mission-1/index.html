CLASS 1

Learn the Science

Start with the job. A structure is successful when its form and materials help it meet the requirements of its function.

🎯FUNCTION

What the structure is meant to do.

🧱FORM

How the structure is organized overall.

🪵MATERIAL

What the parts are made from and what properties those materials bring.

✅PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENT

Something the structure must be able to do safely and reliably.

THREE USEFUL STRUCTURAL FORMS

FRAME

A skeleton of connected members carries the loads.

SHELL

A thin outer surface encloses space and carries loads through its surface.

FRAME + SHELL

A supporting frame works together with an outer covering or surface.

Natural structures do not always fit a human-made category perfectly. Use the forms as tools for noticing how parts support a job—not as labels you must force onto every example.

FAILURE POINTA location or feature where a structure may be more likely to deform, separate, tip or fail. Mission 1 only asks you to notice possible weak points. Mission 2 will explain the forces in detail.
STRUCTURES ACROSS TIME & CULTURE

One authentic Siksika comparison

5–10 MINUTES

Use only what Blackfoot Crossing Historical Park says in its own cultural and architectural materials. Focus on structural science. Do not copy sacred designs or invent cultural meanings.

AUTHENTIC SIKSIKA SOURCE A · BLACKFOOT CROSSING HISTORICAL PARK

Siksika / Blackfoot tipi

Blackfoot Crossing explains that Blackfoot tipis used a four-pole framework and a covering with an opening at the top. When people travelled, the tipi could be collapsed and transported; earlier forms used lighter poles and other coverings.

AUTHENTIC SIKSIKA SOURCE B · BLACKFOOT CROSSING HISTORICAL PARK

Blackfoot Crossing Historical Centre

Blackfoot Crossing describes the centre roof as a semicircular arbour made with tall structural-steel poles. The design intentionally recalls lodgepole pine poles used in tipis; other steel elements reference bow strings and travois.

SAME JOB · DIFFERENT DESIGN

Which storage structure better meets the requirement?

A · CLOSED CARDBOARD BOX

Thin shell surrounds the contents. Good at containing small loose objects, but airflow and visibility are limited.

B · OPEN FRAME BASKET / RACK

Open frame supports contents. Air can move through and contents are visible, but tiny loose objects may escape.

If the requirement is “contain many tiny loose parts,” which design fits better? If the requirement is “maximize airflow and visibility,” which fits better? Same function does not mean one design is always best.
