PHASE 4 · INTEGRATED MATH + SCIENCE IMPLEMENTATIONProject New Horizon — JCEC CityScape Prototype ChallengeThe daily guide below remains the Science spine: 18 core classes + 7 purposeful flex periods. CityScape is the physical project thread that develops across those five missions while selected Math periods handle building geometry, geometric constructions and resource mathematics.
Packet is the record source of truth: use the Field File Team Records for collection/assessment; the existing on-screen Team Record boxes remain useful planning/reference prompts. Printing: one 11-page team set per group + Pages 3, 6 and 10 individually per student. Math boundary: Pages 3, 6 and 10 are Math evidence completed alongside the project; do not turn the Science mission pages into Math lessons or add those periods to the 18 + 7 Science schedule. Primary Building: only one of the three district buildings receives the complete force/material/stability/redesign Science investigation. Fair testing: the Primary Building is the default Mission 4 test object when a fair comparison is possible. If team buildings are not comparable, use the standardized reference model / fallback dataset, then apply the result to the CityScape. Mission 5: no new physical build is required; use the completed district as an evidence object during Structural Design Review. Structural authorization is not the final settlement decision.

Mission 1 — Read the Building Site

What Makes a Structure Successful?

Student page ↗

BEFORE CLASSAssign new Phase 4 teams/continents; open the Phase 4 Hub; gather 4–5 ordinary structure examples or print evidence cards.

Mission 2 — Trace the Forces

What Pushes and Pulls a Structure?

Student page ↗

BEFORE CLASSPrepare mass-vs-force visual; set one force-measurement station if a spring scale exists; print supplied force data as backup.

MATERIALSString; paper/card; small safe object; 1–3 shared spring scales if available; force-data card.

STUDENTS SEE / DOMass vs force → N vs g/kg → external load vs internal force → begin four force patterns.

KEY SCIENCELoads are external pushes/pulls; structural parts develop internal forces. Mass and force are different measurements.

What Makes a Material Useful?

Student page ↗

CONTINGENCY PLAYBOOK

Recover without buying more equipment

No spring scale available

Use the supplied mass/force readings or one teacher demonstration. The outcome is distinguishing mass/force and reading evidence, not owning a scale.

Missing station materials

Replace the physical station with the matching observation/evidence card. Keep the same reasoning question.

Noisy or contradictory material-test data

Keep the data visible. Pool class results, compare with backup data, and ask which uncontrolled variable or measurement issue could explain the discrepancy.

Uneven material samples

Pre-trim a small standardized set or switch to the backup dataset. Do not pretend unequal samples are a fair material comparison.

Student absent during an investigation

Give class/backup data and require the same interpretation/reasoning. Do not recreate a private full lab.

A class is lost

Cut presentations, extra examples and polish before cutting the scientific decision or Team Record. Use the nearest flex day.

Mission 3 testing runs long

Test fewer materials/loads and pool data. Protect operational definitions and one useful comparison.

Graphing takes too long

Provide a partially completed or teacher-generated graph; students interpret the pattern and limitation.

Baseline stability models behave differently

Treat the variation as evidence about uncontrolled variables; use one teacher reference model or the fallback dataset for comparison.

A model fails before meaningful data are collected

Stop safely, identify why evidence is insufficient, then use the shared/fallback dataset. Do not reward destruction.

Students begin competing for maximum load

Stop the test at the posted ceiling and redirect to evidence quality, alternatives and explanation. There is no strongest-model prize.

Mission 4 modification/retest runs long

Teacher provides two improvement choices; teams choose one, justify it and use supplied retest evidence if needed.

Mission 5 Design Review runs long

Narrow the toolkit and require the six review criteria plus one revision; move final prose to Flex Day 22.

Printing/device failure

Project shared materials, copy templates to the board, or use notebook paper. No core task depends on individual devices.
