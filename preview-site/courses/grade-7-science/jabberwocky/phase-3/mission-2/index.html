CLASSES 1–2

Learn the Science

One question. Three pathways. All three can happen in the same habitat at the same time.

🤝CONDUCTION

Thermal energy transfers through interactions between particles in matter. It is especially important when materials are touching and is easy to observe in solids.

It does not happen only in solids.
↺CONVECTION

Thermal energy is transferred by the movement of a fluid. Fluids include liquids and gases.

Warmer fluid becomes less dense and moves → cooler fluid moves in → circulation can form.
☀️RADIATION

Thermal energy can travel by electromagnetic radiation. Radiation does not need matter between the source and receiver.

Sunlight is the clearest example.
TOUCHING MATERIALS→ CONDUCTION
MOVING LIQUID / GAS→ CONVECTION
ENERGY ACROSS SPACE→ RADIATION
cooler air moves in ↓↺ AIR IS A FLUID ↻warm air moves ↑
Need Help? / Learn More

Conduction: particles transfer energy through collisions and vibrations. A material can conduct energy even if the material itself does not move from place to place.

Convection: part of a liquid or gas moves, carrying thermal energy with it. Density helps create the circulation, but you do not need density equations here.

Radiation: energy can travel through empty space. That is how energy from the Sun reaches Jabberwocky.

Real habitats: walls can conduct, moving air can convect and sunlight can radiate—all at once.

MAIN INVESTIGATION

Heat Pathway Stations

TEAM WORK

Rotate through the three shared stations. At every station answer the same two questions:

Where did thermal energy move?What evidence shows that it moved?
Shared supplies: cupswarm watermetal / plastic / wood piecesfood colouring if useddark / light papershared thermometerssunlight or an existing lamp if available
Safety:Warm water only—no boiling water. Do not use your skin as a thermometer. Follow teacher directions around lamps and glassware.
A
STATION A

Conduction

Place equal lengths of metal, plastic and wood into the same warm-water source. Use the teacher-approved setup to observe or measure how quickly the upper part of each material warms.

Look for:Which material transfers thermal energy along itself most noticeably?
B
STATION B

Convection

Observe a safe warm/cool coloured-water setup or teacher demonstration. Watch how the fluid moves and where the warmer and cooler portions travel.

Remember:Air is also a fluid. The small air-convection visual above is enough; this is not a second major experiment.
C
STATION C

Radiation

Compare dark/light or exposed/shaded surfaces under the same sunlight or shared lamp. Use equal time and distance. Record temperature evidence if suitable thermometers are available.

Look for:Can energy arrive even though the source is not touching the surface?

No-purchase fallback: teacher demonstration + observation cards
CONDUCTION CARD

Equal metal, plastic and wood pieces were placed into the same warm water. Over the same time, the upper part of the metal warmed most noticeably. The materials were not carried from one place to another.

CONVECTION CARD

A small amount of coloured warm water rose and spread while cooler water moved in to replace it. A circulating pattern formed in the fluid.

RADIATION CARD

Two surfaces were placed the same distance from a light source for the same time. The more exposed surface gained more thermal energy even though the source did not touch it.

If any physical station cannot be run, use the matching observation card. The goal is to reason from evidence, not to purchase specialty equipment.

Low-material rule:Normal classroom supplies and shared stations are enough. No infrared camera, specialty heat-transfer kit or purchased sensor is required.
