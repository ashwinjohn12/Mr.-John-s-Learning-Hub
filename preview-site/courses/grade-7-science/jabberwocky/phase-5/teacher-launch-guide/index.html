TEACHER SYSTEM // PHASE 5

THE DEEP RECORD

JCEC GEOLOGICAL SURVEY DIVISION · TEACHER LAUNCH GUIDE

Complete implementation system for the five-mission student experience. 18 core classes + 7 purposeful flex periods = 25 classes.

Open student hub →Open Mission 1 →Open released Council →
LOW-MATERIAL RULEordinary classroom supplies → shared/reused equipment → printables/data fallbacks → specialty equipment only as optional enrichment
PHASE BOUNDARYPhase 5 ends with a geological evidence handoff. Do not begin the Council before the Geological Evidence Packet and reflection are complete. After that, keep the same Phase 5 team and continent and continue to the released Mission 2190 Council.

DAY 1
Mission 1 — Read the Ground

How Do Geologists Know?

Student route ↗
BEFORE CLASS
Open the Phase 5 Hub; assign/confirm the final Geological Survey continent; preload the Earth Reference Evidence strips; decide whether physical samples or evidence cards will be used on Day 2.
MATERIALS
Devices/projector; Phase 5 posting; Previous Team Geological Briefing; Earth-interior/tool visuals.
STUDENTS SEE / DO
Phase launch → new posting → four-card Geological Briefing → Earth Reference vs Jabberwocky Survey Evidence → observation/property/rock/mineral/classification → layered Earth + evidence/tool preview → gradual/sudden-change preview.
KEY SCIENCE
Geology begins with evidence. Scientists use direct and indirect observations to classify materials and build models of Earth.
TEACHER EMPHASIS
Do not turn Day 1 into rock-name memorization. Keep Earth examples separate from Jabberwocky canon.
COLLECT / ASSESS
Quick observation-vs-inference check — formative.
IF TIME RUNS OUT
Use one evidence-source example, one tool example and the broad crust/mantle/core model. Protect the evidence-source distinction.

Mission 3 — Track the Changing Surface

Break, Move, Settle

Student route ↗
BEFORE CLASS
Prepare weathering evidence cards; choose the tray/sediment setup and one standard water amount for Day 9; display fair-test variable plan.
MATERIALS
Weathering cards; tray setup preview; rulers; data sheet; towels.
STUDENTS SEE / DO
Weathering vs erosion vs deposition → gradual vs sudden change → fair-test question/prediction → identify change/measure/keep-similar variables.
KEY SCIENCE
Weathering breaks material down; erosion moves it; deposition/sedimentation settles transported material.

DAY 9
Mission 3 — Track the Changing Surface

Surface Change Fair Test

Student route ↗
BEFORE CLASS
Set shallow trays; pre-measure small sediment amounts; mark one 50–100 mL water amount; place devices away from water; prepare fallback dataset.
MATERIALS
Reusable trays; small sand/soil/gravel amount; marked cup/bottle; ruler; books/blocks; towels; data table; backup dataset.
STUDENTS SEE / DO
Run lower-slope vs higher-slope comparison → measure sediment transport distance → record deposition location + observation.
KEY SCIENCE
A fair test changes one important variable while keeping other conditions reasonably similar; evidence can be quantitative and qualitative.
TEACHER EMPHASIS
No erosion contest. One water manager per team. Stop and switch to data cards if setup becomes unsafe or inconsistent.
COLLECT / ASSESS
Surface Change data — checkpoint.
IF TIME RUNS OUT
Run one team/class demonstration and give all groups the shared dataset.
DAY 10
Mission 3 — Track the Changing Surface

What Does the Pattern Show?

Student route ↗
BEFORE CLASS
Pool selected class data; prepare the supplied anomaly case and the short interpolation/extrapolation training sequence.
MATERIALS
Class/fallback dataset; table/graph template if used; anomaly prompt; model-data task; continent clues.
STUDENTS SEE / DO
Identify overall pattern → explain one discrepant/anomalous result → interpolate/extrapolate model data → discuss why real landscapes may not change at a constant rate → read continent clues.
KEY SCIENCE

TEACHER EMPHASIS

COLLECT / ASSESS
Pattern/anomaly/prediction reasoning — checkpoint.

Contingency playbook

No rock/mineral samples

Run Mission 1 and Mission 2 entirely from the built-in photo/property/evidence cards. The learning target is evidence reasoning, not owning a collection.

Limited hand lenses

Rotate the few lenses at one station, use device zoom/projected close-ups, or omit lenses entirely when the property data card already provides the needed observation.

Students over-identify samples from one clue

Return to ONE CLUE ≠ PROOF. Require at least two observations or allow ROCK / POSSIBLE MINERAL / UNCERTAIN / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE YET.

Hardness testing becomes confusing

Stop sample testing and switch to teacher-provided relative-hardness results. Students interpret the evidence rather than troubleshoot tools.

Mission 2 rock classifications are ambiguous

Treat ambiguity as expected. Use “consistent with” language and keep the uncertainty field; do not force a named rock or class.

Local Alberta evidence unavailable

Use the cached teacher summary from the existing AGS source or postpone the 5–10 minute local card to a flex period. Do not replace it with invented Jabberwocky geology.

Sediment/tray materials unavailable

Use the complete supplied Surface Change dataset, before/after diagrams and anomaly task.

Surface Change Fair Test runs long

Run one trial per condition, pool class evidence, or finish physical testing and use the fallback dataset for analysis. Protect the reasoning rather than extra trials.

Water spills/device concerns

Stop devices at wet stations; use printed data sheets; wipe spills immediately; switch remaining teams to the dataset if needed.

Groups get contradictory erosion data

Keep contradictions visible. Compare pouring rate, starting sediment shape, side-flow and measurement differences; then use pooled/fallback data for the main pattern.

Graph/data interpretation takes too long

Provide a teacher-prepared graph/table and assess pattern, anomaly, prediction and limitation instead of graph polish.

Student absent during the fair test

Use class/supplied evidence and require the same CLAIM → DATA → PROCESS → PREDICTION → LIMITATION reasoning.

Students erase anomalous results

Pause and model why anomalous data are scientifically useful. Require the odd result plus one plausible explanation.

Students over-extrapolate model data

Attach the phrase “if model conditions stayed similar” to every prediction and ask what could change in a real landscape.

Mission 4 vocabulary becomes overwhelming

Collapse the visible focus to layers → bend/break → fossils → time → uncertainty; keep advanced terms behind teacher explanation.

Students confuse fracture with fault

Repeat: a fracture is a break; a fault requires evidence of movement. Use Slithy Toves as the explicit non-example.

Students assume Earth-reference volcanoes/earthquakes exist on Jabberwocky

Point to the evidence-source labels. Earth Reference Evidence teaches meaning; Jabberwocky Survey Evidence establishes canon.

Students treat fossils as dinosaur trivia

Ask “What can this evidence tell us about past life/environment, and what can it not tell us alone?” Require context/layer discussion.

Students struggle with relative sequencing

Use the common Earth Training Cross-Section first; physically order event cards; keep only four rules and no absolute ages.

Printing/projector failure

Read evidence cards aloud, draw the simplified cross-section/table on the board, and have teams use notebook paper for Team Records.

Mission 5 Evidence Locker becomes an answer key

Call each item a reminder, not a final answer. Students must choose three, label which are observation vs inference, and may replace them with stronger actual classroom evidence.

Students confuse the geological handoff with the final settlement decision

Display THIS IS NOT THE FINAL HUMAN-SETTLEMENT DECISION. Ask only what geological guidance the future Council needs.

A class is lost

Cut extra examples, presentations and polish before cutting the investigation reasoning, Team Record or reflection. Use the nearest flex period.

Teacher launch-readiness audit

Do I know what to prepare tomorrow?Yes — every core day has a BEFORE CLASS field and route.
Do I know what students should finish each day?Yes — each core day names the evidence/product to collect or protect.
Can I run the unit with no geology purchases?Yes — Missions 1–2 can be card/photo based; Mission 3 uses ordinary trays/sediment/water or supplied data; Missions 4–5 are paper/projector first.
Do I know which activities actually need physical materials?Yes — only the optional real-sample stations and Surface Change Fair Test benefit meaningfully from physical materials; both have complete evidence-card/data fallbacks.
Do I know what is formative, checkpoint and major assessment?Yes — M1/M2 formative, M3 checkpoint, M4 formative synthesis, M5 major synthesis.
Can I recover if a lab, dataset, sample set or class goes wrong?Yes — the contingency playbook and seven flex periods protect every core reasoning target.
Do Missions 1–5 remain scientifically distinct?Yes — observe/classify → formation history → ongoing surface change → reconstruct deep past → geological handoff.
Does the teacher system preserve evidence-first geology?Yes — sample naming is secondary to observations, processes, patterns, uncertainty and evidence-based explanations.
Do Earth Reference Evidence and Jabberwocky Survey Evidence stay separate?Yes — Earth examples teach what evidence can mean; only JCEC survey observations establish Jabberwocky claims.
Does Mission 5 still stop before the final Mission 2190 Council decision?Yes — Mission 5 ends with the geological handoff only. After the packet and reflection are complete, students continue to the separately released Council route.
Do I know how Phase 5 hands off to the Council?Yes — keep the same Phase 5 team and continent; Mission 5 provides the Council transition after the Geological Evidence Packet and reflection are complete.
Do teacher and student systems match?Yes — mission titles, pacing, investigations, Team Records, assessment roles, evidence lockers and Council handoff are aligned.
