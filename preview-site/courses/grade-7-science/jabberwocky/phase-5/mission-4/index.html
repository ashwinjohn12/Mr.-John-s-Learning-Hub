CLASSES 1–2

Learn the Science

The goal is not to memorize a wall of geology words. Use each idea as a clue in one reasoning pattern:

OBSERVATION→PATTERN→SCIENTIFIC EXPLANATION
STRATA / LAYERS

Sequences of deposited material can preserve evidence of earlier conditions.

FOLDING

Rock layers can bend. The pattern can provide evidence that layers were changed after they formed.

FAULTING

Rock can break and move along a fracture. A fracture is not automatically a fault unless movement is supported by evidence.

CRUSTAL MOVEMENT

Large-scale movement of Earth’s crust helps explain patterns in mountains, folds, faults, earthquakes and volcanoes.

FOSSIL EVIDENCE

Preserved remains, traces or impressions can provide evidence of past life and environments.

GEOLOGICAL TIME

Earth history is extremely long. Scientists organize that history into divisions using accumulated evidence.

UNCERTAINTY

One piece of evidence rarely tells the whole geological story. Several agreeing clues make an explanation stronger.

Need Help? / Grade 7 Scope

This mission stays qualitative. Radiometric-dating calculations, seismic-wave mathematics, detailed plate vectors and advanced tectonic terminology are outside the required pathway. You are using visible patterns and relative sequence to build an evidence-based explanation.

EARTH REFERENCE EVIDENCE

Three short files. Three ways evidence builds an explanation.

REAL EARTH
FILE A · CANADIAN ROCKIES / CORDILLERA

Folded + faulted rock can record crustal change.

Parks Canada describes the Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks as highly faulted, folded and uplifted sedimentary rocks. The important Grade 7 idea is the pattern: layers formed first, then later crustal forces changed their position and shape.

Parks Canada source ↗
FILE B · GLOBAL CRUSTAL-MOVEMENT PATTERNS

Patterns of earthquakes and volcanoes help reveal moving plates.

The USGS world map places plate boundaries, earthquakes and volcanoes together. Students use the spatial pattern as evidence that Earth’s crust is divided into moving plates—without memorizing detailed boundary categories.

USGS map/source ↗
FILE C · ALBERTA BADLANDS / ROYAL TYRRELL

Layers + erosion + fossils preserve a record of changing environments.

The Royal Tyrrell’s Grade 7-connected badlands material links sedimentation, erosion and fossilization to interpreting Alberta’s past. Fossils make the most scientific sense when their geological context is preserved.

FOSSIL EVIDENCE

What can a fossil tell us—and what can it NOT tell us by itself?

CONTEXT MATTERS
BODY FOSSIL

Preserved remains or parts of an organism.

TRACE FOSSIL

Evidence of activity such as a track, burrow or other trace.

MOLD / CAST-TYPE EVIDENCE

An impression left in material, or material that later fills that impression.

Fossilization requires particular conditions, so the fossil record is incomplete. One fossil can be useful evidence, but its layer, location and surrounding evidence matter.

Alberta stewardship: the Royal Tyrrell Museum explains that location is scientifically important; buried fossils should be left in place and reported rather than excavated by the public. Follow the Museum/provincial guidance for Alberta finds.

GEOLOGICAL TIME

Organize a very long history without a giant-number test.

NO AGE CALCULATIONS
PRECAMBRIAN

Very long early history of Earth.

PALEOZOIC

Major diversification of ancient life.

MESOZOIC

Includes the age of dinosaurs and many other organisms.

CENOZOIC

Most recent major division of geological time.

Scientists organize Earth history into broad divisions using accumulated rock and fossil evidence. You do not need to memorize numerical boundaries or calculate ages in millions/billions of years.

CLASS 3

Investigate

MAIN INVESTIGATION

Deep Record Evidence Puzzle

RELATIVE SEQUENCE · NO ABSOLUTE AGES

Question: What happened first, next, later and most recently—and which parts of the history remain uncertain?

RULE 1In an undisturbed sequence, lower deposited layers are generally older than layers above them.
RULE 2A fold or fault happened after the layers it affected already existed.
RULE 3An erosion surface formed after the material it cuts or removes.
RULE 4A later layer/deposit formed after the surface below it.
