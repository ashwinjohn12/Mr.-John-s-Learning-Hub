CLASS 1

Learn the Science

Start with four plant structures. Each structure has a job.

🪴Roots

Absorb water and materials from the environment and anchor the plant.

🌿Stem

Supports the plant and helps move water and other materials.

🍃Leaves

Capture light and help with photosynthesis and gas exchange.

🌼Flowers / reproductive structures

Help seed plants reproduce and make the next generation.

AdaptationA structure or behaviour that helps an organism survive in its environment.
Why plants matter

Plants make food, support food webs, create habitat and provide materials humans use. A useful plant is still part of an ecosystem.

Need help? Match structure to job

Roots: think absorb + anchor.

Stem: think support + transport.

Leaves: think light + food making + gas exchange.

Flowers / reproductive structures: think reproduction + seeds.

CLASS 1

Plant Structure Investigation

TEAM WORK

Question: How does each plant structure help a real plant survive?

You need:one teacher-approved flowering/seed planthand lens if availablefield sheet
  1. Observe the whole plant before touching or separating anything.
  2. Find the roots, stem, leaves and flower/reproductive structure.
  3. Sketch or label each structure on your field sheet.
  4. For each structure, write its main job.
  5. Choose one structure and explain how its shape or features may help the plant survive.
Look closely first.Structure → job → survival.

Gyre · Frumious Basin

Skyroot

Starchy food from enlarged roots and coarse fibre from tough stems.

1RootA thick storage root grows deep into narrow pockets of soil.

JOB: Stores food and water while anchoring the plant on steep ground.

2StemShort flexible stems stay close to the ground.

JOB: Reduces damage from strong alpine winds.

3LeavesSmall waxy, slightly hairy leaves cover the plant.

JOB: Helps reduce water loss and protect leaf surfaces from cold wind.

4Flower / seed structureSmall dark flowers open quickly during the warmest part of the short summer.

JOB: Allows reproduction during a brief growing season.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Low growth, storage roots and protected leaves help Skyroot survive cold, windy mountain conditions.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Cold temperatures, thin soils and very little flat land limit cultivation.

Sustainability warning: Large-scale digging or cultivation could loosen thin mountain soils and increase erosion.

🌿
Brillig · Tulgey Station

Rainspout Tree

Nutritious fruit and seeds plus strong flexible bark fibre.

1RootWide surface roots spread around the trunk and rise above wet ground.

JOB: Supports the tall tree in shallow, rain-soaked soil.

2StemA tall woody trunk lifts the leaves toward canopy light.

JOB: Supports the crown and transports water and materials through the tree.

3LeavesBroad leaves end in narrow drip tips.

JOB: Captures light while allowing heavy rain to drain from the leaf surface.

4Flower / seed structureClusters of flowers and fruit grow high in the canopy.

JOB: Produces seeds while attracting local pollinators and fruit-eating organisms.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Broad drip-tip leaves and spreading support roots fit Brillig’s wet forest environment.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Useful nutrients are tied up in living vegetation rather than deep fertile soil.

Sustainability warning: Clearing forest for plantations could damage the canopy, increase erosion and remove habitat.

🏝️
Manxome · Calloo Island

Storm Palm

Edible fruit, oil-rich seeds and long fibres for cordage or woven material.

1RootA dense mat of fibrous roots spreads through shallow coastal soil.

JOB: Anchors the plant and helps hold soil during storms.

2StemThe trunk bends noticeably without snapping.

JOB: Allows the plant to flex during strong winds.

3LeavesLong narrow leaf sections bend and stream with the wind.

JOB: Reduces the force of storm winds on the plant.

4Flower / seed structureFlowers develop into clusters of floating, salt-tolerant fruits.

JOB: Produces seeds that can disperse between islands.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Flexible stems, fibrous roots and wind-tolerant leaves help Storm Palm survive coastal storms.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Storms and salt exposure can damage young plants and growing areas.

Sustainability warning: Large uniform groves could be vulnerable to one storm or pest and replace diverse island habitat.

🏜️
Slithy Toves · Borogove Station

Reservoir Thorn

Oil-rich seeds, edible inner tissue and tough fibre.

1RootA very deep main root reaches moisture far below the surface.

JOB: Accesses water that is unavailable near the dry surface.

2StemA thick green stem stores water after rare rainfall.

JOB: Stores water and can also capture light when leaves are reduced.

3LeavesTiny leaves appear briefly and many branches end in sharp thorns.

JOB: Small leaves reduce water loss while thorns protect stored water-rich tissue.

4Flower / seed structureFlowers open quickly after rainfall and produce hard-coated seeds.

JOB: Allows reproduction during short wet periods and helps seeds survive dry times.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Deep roots, water storage and reduced leaves help Reservoir Thorn survive extreme drought.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Irrigation would compete for scarce fresh water and may increase salt buildup in soil.

Sustainability warning: Expanding production could use water needed by native ecosystems and worsen salinization.

❄️
Wabe · Beamish Station

Ember Moss

Oil-rich seeds and soft insulating fibre from dense mats.

🌼 Important: Despite its common name, Ember Moss is a flowering seed plant—not a true moss.

1RootShallow roots spread through the thin thawed surface layer.

JOB: Collects water and anchors the plant without needing deep unfrozen soil.

2StemShort stems form dense mats close to the ground.

JOB: Keeps the plant in a slightly warmer, less windy layer of air.

3LeavesSmall dark leaves absorb sunlight and cluster tightly together.

JOB: Helps capture light while reducing exposure to cold wind.

4Flower / seed structureTiny flowers appear and produce seeds rapidly during midsummer.

JOB: Completes reproduction during the short growing season.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Low mat growth, shallow roots and rapid flowering fit Wabe’s cold, short growing season.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Natural growth is slow and large yields may require protected or heated growing spaces.

Sustainability warning: Harvesting natural mats faster than they regrow could damage slowly recovering habitat.

🌾
Bandersnatch · Jubjub Station

Goldstem Grain

High-energy grain plus straw-like fibre.

1RootA dense fibrous root system fills the upper soil.

JOB: Absorbs water quickly and helps hold prairie soil in place.

2StemHollow flexible stems bend during strong winds.

JOB: Supports seed heads while reducing breakage.

3LeavesLong narrow leaves grow from several levels of the stem.

JOB: Captures light while limiting water loss in windy conditions.

4Flower / seed structureMany small flowers form a compact grain head.

JOB: Produces many seeds in one growing season.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Fibrous roots, narrow leaves and rapid seed production fit the open seasonal prairie.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Conditions are favourable enough that humans may be tempted to cultivate very large areas.

Sustainability warning: Monoculture and habitat conversion could reduce biodiversity and increase soil erosion.

🌲
Gimble · Galumph Station

Ironwood

Very strong timber and durable plant fibre.

1RootLarge spreading roots interlock with forest soil.

JOB: Anchors the tree and helps stabilize soil around the trunk.

2StemA thick woody trunk grows slowly and becomes extremely strong.

JOB: Supports the canopy and stores a large amount of structural material.

3LeavesBroad seasonal leaves form a layered canopy.

JOB: Captures light during the growing season.

4Flower / seed structureSmall flowers produce heavy winged seeds after many years of growth.

JOB: Allows reproduction, but new generations establish slowly.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Strong wood, broad leaves and extensive roots fit a long-lived forest tree.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Ironwood grows slowly, so harvested trees take many years to replace.

Sustainability warning: Large-scale logging could fragment forest habitat and increase erosion.

🪷
Mimsy · Mome Station

Floatroot

Edible seeds and flexible fibres for woven or lightweight materials.

1RootFine roots hang in saturated mud and shallow water.

JOB: Absorbs materials while tolerating very wet soil.

2StemHollow stems contain large air spaces.

JOB: Moves air through the plant and helps support tissues in waterlogged conditions.

3LeavesBroad waxy leaves float or rise just above the water.

JOB: Keeps leaf surfaces in the light and air.

4Flower / seed structureFlowers rise above the water and later release buoyant seeds.

JOB: Allows pollination and helps seeds spread through wetlands.

BIG ADAPTATION IDEA

Air-filled stems, wet-tolerant roots and floating leaves help Floatroot survive saturated wetlands.

Learn More: future production challenge

Growing challenge: Conventional fields would require drainage, while contained or raised systems need careful design.

Sustainability warning: Draining wetlands for agriculture would damage habitat and increase flood risk.
