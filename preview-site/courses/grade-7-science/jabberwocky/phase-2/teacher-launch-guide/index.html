DAY 1
WEEK 1 · Mission 1 — Find the Living Resource

New Team, New Resource Plant

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Assign Phase 2 teams/continents; open the Phase 2 Hub; set up 3–5 shared flowering/seed-plant stations; print Plant Structure Investigation sheets.

MATERIALS

Devices; a few shared real flowering/seed plants; pencils; Plant Structure Investigation sheet. Hand lenses are optional; trays are only needed if the specimens are messy.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Phase 2 launch → new-team/new-continent handoff → Previous Team Briefing → roots, stems, leaves, reproductive structures → begin real-plant investigation.

KEY SCIENCE

Plant structures have functions: roots absorb/anchor; stems support/transport; leaves capture light and support photosynthesis/gas exchange; reproductive structures help make the next generation.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Use structure → job → survival. Rotate teams through shared plant stations rather than buying one specimen per team.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Plant Structure Investigation observations — formative only.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Extended whole-class sharing. Finish the structure-to-job table next class if needed.

DAY 2
WEEK 1 · Mission 1 — Find the Living Resource

Earth Plant → Jabberwocky Plant

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Confirm Phase 2 continent postings are saved; return field sheets; open continent resource-plant clues.

MATERIALS

Devices; Mission 1 field sheet; Living Resource Profile; pencils.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Finish real-plant reasoning → investigate four Jabberwocky structure clues → connect structure, function, adaptation and possible human use.

KEY SCIENCE

Plant structures vary; adaptations can help plants survive particular environments; useful plants remain part of ecosystems.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

The fictional plant must obey the science students observed. Keep evidence ahead of imagination.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Check at least one structure claim is supported by a clue — formative.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Optional future-production details and artistic polish.

DAY 3
WEEK 1 · Mission 1 — Find the Living Resource

Living Resource Decision

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Make the Living Resource Profile available; confirm inherited Phase 1 warning is visible.

MATERIALS

Device; Living Resource Profile; Mission 1 evidence.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Choose the plant feature that makes the resource most promising or difficult for humans to use → complete one profile + reflection.

KEY SCIENCE

Adaptations affect both survival and possible human use.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

One strong feature with a clear explanation is enough. Assess reasoning, not drawing quality.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Living Resource Profile — formative; individual reflection for feedback.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Sharing/polish. Keep the team decision, profile and reflection.

DAY 4
WEEK 1 · Mission 2 — Keep It Growing

What Does a Plant Need?

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Choose ONE class-wide changed condition. Prefer a no-purchase condition such as reduced water or reduced light. Stage two small reused containers/team, seeds, growing medium and labels; start one teacher backup tray beforehand if practical.

MATERIALS

2 small reused containers/team; fast-growing seeds; growing medium; labels; water/measuring cup; tray or spill mat; Growth Trial data sheet. Extra treatment materials are only needed if you deliberately choose a treatment that requires them.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Mission launch → basic plant needs → set up CONTROL and CHANGED conditions → record starting state.

KEY SCIENCE

Plant growth depends on environmental conditions; fair tests change one condition while keeping others similar.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Use one class-wide changed condition so pooled evidence is useful. Clean yogurt cups or similar reused containers are fine—the goal is evidence, not specialized equipment.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Growth Trial setup/data sheet — formative evidence, not a separate assignment.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Teacher can finish watering/placement logistics after class; do not skip labels or fair-test thinking.

DAY 5
WEEK 1 · Mission 2 — Keep It Growing

Get + Move Water and Materials

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Check Growth Trial containers; prepare a short coloured-water transport/transpiration demo only if useful.

MATERIALS

Devices; Growth Trial sheet; ruler; optional clear cup + coloured water + celery/carnation or transpiration demo.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

GET MATERIALS: diffusion + osmosis → MOVE WATER & MATERIALS: transport + transpiration → 3–5 minute Growth Trial check.

KEY SCIENCE

Water and dissolved materials enter/move through plants; water can leave leaves as vapour.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Teach one connected pathway, not four disconnected vocabulary words. Detailed xylem/phloem is optional.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Quick observation + exit explanation — formative.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Optional demonstration before cutting the connected process explanation.

DAY 6
WEEK 2 · Mission 2 — Keep It Growing

Make Food + Make New Plants

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Check plants; prepare a simple life-cycle visual.

MATERIALS

Devices; Growth Trial sheet; ruler; simple seed/seedling/flower images or specimens.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Photosynthesis + gas exchange → seed → germination → seedling → mature plant → flowers/seeds → simple propagation → quick Growth Trial check.

KEY SCIENCE

Plants use light, water and carbon dioxide to make food; oxygen is released; seed plants reproduce and new plants can begin from seeds or some vegetative methods.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Keep photosynthesis conceptual. Do not add detailed cellular chemistry or Grade 9 genetics.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Quick process explanation — formative.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Shorten propagation examples; protect photosynthesis, gas exchange and the life-cycle sequence.

DAY 7
WEEK 2 · Mission 2 — Keep It Growing

What Will Work on Jabberwocky?

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Confirm continent growth clues are available; prepare Plant Growth Requirements Card.

MATERIALS

Device; Growth Trial sheet; Plant Growth Requirements Card.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Compare available Growth Trial evidence → read assigned continent clues → choose the growing condition humans must control most carefully → card + reflection.

KEY SCIENCE

Plant processes interact with environmental conditions; growth needs vary by context.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Use: When ___ changed, ___ changed. This suggests the plant needs ___. On our continent, this matters because ___.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Plant Growth Requirements Card — formative. Keep Growth Trial running.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Finish the card on Flex Day 8 if needed; do not end the Growth Trial just because Mission 2 is complete.

DAY 9
WEEK 2 · Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone

What Makes Good Soil?

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Set up shared stations with small class quantities of sand-rich, clay-rich and organic-rich media. Reuse identical cups/filters or drainage containers at the stations.

MATERIALS

Three shared media; reusable drainage cups/containers; trays; water; measuring cups; spoons; one or a few timers; paper towels; investigation sheet.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Mission launch → particle size → pore spaces → drainage/retention/compaction → begin Soil / Growing Medium Investigation.

KEY SCIENCE

Particle size and pore spaces affect water movement and root conditions.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Make the causal chain explicit: particles → spaces → water movement → root conditions. Teams rotate through stations rather than receiving three full material sets.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Investigation observations — formative.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Finish drainage measurements next class; remove extra media rather than core comparisons.

DAY 10
WEEK 2 · Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone

Investigate the Growing Medium

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Reset shared media stations if needed; prepare short examples of fertilizer, irrigation, protected growing, raised/contained beds and soilless growing.

MATERIALS

Shared soil-investigation stations; technology cards/images; devices.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Complete media tests → compare evidence → connect fertilizer, irrigation and modified growing environments to problems they solve and impacts they may create.

KEY SCIENCE

Plants need water and dissolved materials; human technologies can modify growing conditions and can create unintended effects.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Technologies are tools for particular problems, not automatically better methods.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Completed soil/growing-medium evidence — formative.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Keep one clear example each of water, nutrients and environment modification; move detail to Learn More.

DAY 11
WEEK 3 · Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone

Build the Growing Zone

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Take final/near-final Growth Trial reading if practical; set out one shared prototype-material station; provide one reused cup/container per team or allow a labelled design instead; confirm continent challenge cards.

MATERIALS

Growth Trial sheets; rulers; one reused cup/container per team if building; shared media/gravel/wick/string/cover/water; safe drainage setup; continent evidence. A labelled design is an equally valid low-material option.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Compare CONTROL vs CHANGED evidence → read 3–4 continent clues → build/test one small growing-system model OR create a labelled testable design solving one problem.

KEY SCIENCE

Plant-needs evidence + media behaviour can guide a practical growing system; solutions can also create consequences.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

One problem, one testable idea, one consequence. Do not make a miniature engineering kit for every team; the reasoning is the checkpoint.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Prototype/design evidence + Growth Trial comparison — checkpoint evidence in progress.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Use a labelled design instead of a physical build; protect evidence-to-decision reasoning.

DAY 12
WEEK 3 · Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone

Recommend a Growing System

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Return soil/Growth Trial evidence; prepare Growing System Recommendation.

MATERIALS

Device; growing-medium evidence; Growth Trial evidence; prototype/design; Growing System Recommendation.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Recommend one system → identify one environmental consequence → add one safeguard → Team Record + reflection.

KEY SCIENCE

A practical growing system should fit plant needs/environmental conditions while reducing foreseeable impacts.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Our system should ___ because evidence shows ___. One possible problem is ___, so we would ___.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Growing System Recommendation — stronger Phase 2 reasoning checkpoint.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Accept a clear diagram + concise reasoning; cut presentation/polish before consequence + safeguard.

DAY 14
WEEK 3 · Mission 4 — Choose the Next Generation

Plants Are Not All the Same

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Print/cut Selective Breeding Simulation starter cards + tracker.

MATERIALS

Starter population cards; trait labels; generation tracker; devices.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Reproduction/pollination → plant variety → selective breeding → choose one target trait → begin simulation.

KEY SCIENCE

Plants of the same kind vary; repeated human selection can make useful characteristics more common in cultivated varieties.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Variation exists before selection. No Punnett squares, alleles, genotype/phenotype or dominant/recessive inheritance.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Simulation tracker — formative/light.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Model one generation together and continue next class.

DAY 15
WEEK 3 · Mission 4 — Choose the Next Generation

Choose the Next Generation

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Prepare continent surprise event cards and short pest-management examples.

MATERIALS

Simulation cards/tracker; surprise cards; biological, physical/cultural and chemical control cards.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Run 2–3 simplified generations → observe trait change → reveal surprise → discuss low-variety/monoculture risk → consider simple control strategies.

KEY SCIENCE

Selective breeding can increase useful traits; uniform populations may share vulnerabilities; pest controls can have benefits and unintended effects.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Selective breeding can be useful. Variety and safeguards still matter. Real offspring vary and real breeding takes many generations.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Simulation reasoning — formative/light.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Stop after two generations; reveal only one surprise.

DAY 18
WEEK 4 · Mission 5 — Use It Without Losing It

How Big Can This Get?

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Print/stage continent Production Footprint maps and paper tiles; confirm continent/resource plant/inherited warning are visible.

MATERIALS

Printed footprint map; paper growing/harvest, protected habitat, water, infrastructure, processing/storage and buffer tiles; devices.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Land use + production inputs + resilience + sustainability → receive 6-zone map, one production goal, 3 constraints → begin resource-use plan.

KEY SCIENCE

Land conversion, irrigation, fertilizer, pest management, infrastructure and harvesting can increase production and affect habitat, soil, water and resilience.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Ask Can we keep producing it without damaging the system? not only Can we produce it? This is intentionally a paper planning model, not a construction project.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Initial footprint plan — formative evidence for final synthesis.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Use fewer tiles; do not add more zones or constraints.

DAY 19
WEEK 4 · Mission 5 — Use It Without Losing It

The Consequence

Open →
BEFORE CLASS

Prepare one continent-appropriate consequence card per team.

MATERIALS

Printed footprint maps/tiles; consequence cards; pencils.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Complete initial plan → reveal consequence → identify what it exposes → revise exactly one part of the plan.

KEY SCIENCE

Resource systems can create unintended effects; sustainable planning includes monitoring, limits, buffers and revision.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

The consequence is not a punishment for a wrong answer. It tests whether the plan can adapt.

COLLECT / ASSESS

One justified revision — formative evidence.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Require one written/diagrammed revision instead of rebuilding the whole map.

Growth Trial timeline

If the plants do not cooperate

Poor germination: use teacher backup plants, pooled class data or a sample dataset. Little difference: treat that as valid evidence and discuss what the data can/cannot support. Slow growth: use the strongest available observations and keep the unit moving.

Keep enrichment optional

Detailed xylem/phloem anatomy, cellular photosynthesis chemistry, advanced soil chemistry, Punnett squares/genetics terminology, detailed pesticide chemistry and agricultural economics are not required on the core student pathway. Hydroponics is one possible growing technology example, not a separate mandatory unit.

RECOVERY PLAN

When real classrooms happen

Failed germination

Use teacher backup plants, pooled class results or a teacher-provided sample dataset. Preserve evidence interpretation; do not restart the whole unit.

Missing lab materials

Use shared stations/demos, two media instead of three, prepared data, digital cards, a labelled growing-system design or paper zone labels. Keep the reasoning target rather than the apparatus.

A class is lost

Use the nearest flex day first. If needed, combine the lightest adjacent tasks and protect each mission’s final evidence-based decision.

Student absent during a lab

Provide team/class data + photo/setup summary + one focused interpretation question. Do not require a solo repeat unless practical.

Growth Trial shows little difference

Use the actual result. Ask what the evidence supports, whether treatment/time was sufficient, and what remains uncertain. Do not invent the expected result.

Selective-breeding simulation runs long

Stop after two generations and reveal one surprise. The target is repeated selection + low-variety risk, not number of rounds.

Production Footprint Challenge runs long

Freeze the current plan, reveal the consequence, and require one written/diagrammed revision instead of rebuilding the entire map.

If students are overloaded, return to one scaffold.

TEACHER LAUNCH-READINESS AUDIT

Can I teach this tomorrow?

Do I know what to prepare tomorrow?

Yes. Every core class names preparation/materials, and the Mission Materials Master List separates shared/reusable resources from print/digital resources.

Do I know what students should finish each day?

Yes. Each day names the student task and collection point. Only Missions 3 and 5 carry significant formal weight.

Do I know what I actually need to assess?

Yes. M1/M2/M4 formative; M3 checkpoint; M5 major synthesis; optional individual science check.

Can I recover if plant growth or lab timing changes?

Yes. Flex Days 8 and 13 protect the Growth Trial/Mission 3, and the contingency plan supports pooled/sample data, shared stations, labelled designs, shorter simulations and written revisions.

Do teacher and student systems match?

Yes. The same mission names, 3 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4 pacing, investigations, Team Records, reasoning scaffolds and final Living Resource Plan are used in both.
