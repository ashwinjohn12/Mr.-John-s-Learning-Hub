CLASSES 1–2

Learn the Science

Think of soil and growing systems as one problem: roots need the right balance of water, air, materials and support.

🪨SOIL PARTICLES

Sand-rich: larger particles and larger spaces. Clay-rich: very small particles and tiny spaces. Organic-rich: contains decomposed plant material that can help hold water and nutrients.

💧WATER IN SOIL

Drainage is how easily water leaves. Retention is how much water stays. Compaction squeezes spaces smaller and can reduce air and water movement.

🧪MATERIALS PLANTS NEED

Roots need water and dissolved nutrients/materials. Fertilizer can add nutrients, but too much can wash away or damage water and soil systems.

🏗️CHANGE THE ENVIRONMENT

Irrigation, protected growing, raised/contained beds and soilless systems can solve particular problems. They are tools—not automatically better choices.

Particle size → pore spaces → water movement → root conditionsThis is the connection to remember—not a long list of soil words.
Learn More: four growing tools

Irrigation: supplies water where or when natural water is not enough.

Protected growing: uses a cover/greenhouse to change temperature, wind or moisture.

Raised / contained bed: gives humans more control over the root zone and drainage.

Soilless / hydroponic growing: grows plants without ordinary soil, but needs controlled water, nutrients and equipment.

CLASSES 1–2

Soil / Growing Medium Investigation

TEAM WORK

Question: Which growing material holds water, drains water, and supports useful root conditions most effectively?

Your teacher provides:3 media at shared stationsshared equal containersequal water amountstimer / measuring cup if availablefield sheet
  1. At the shared station, compare sand-rich, clay-rich and organic-rich samples (or the teacher-provided equivalents).
  2. Observe texture and particle size. Gently press each sample once to compare how easily it compacts.
  3. Use the same amount of each material in similar shared containers.
  4. Add the same amount of water. Record how quickly or how much drains through.
  5. Compare what remains damp, what drains fastest and what became most compact. Decide what each material might be useful for.
Fair comparison:Use equal amounts of material and water, similar containers and the same procedure.

Gyre · Skyroot

Cold conditions, thin soils and very little flat growing land

1SOILNatural growing pockets are shallow and rocky.

WHY IT MATTERS: A contained growing bed may need deeper material without stripping fragile slopes.

2TEMPERATUREThe useful growing season is short and cold nights can return quickly.

WHY IT MATTERS: Protection from cold could extend the time plants can grow.

3WATERMountain soil can drain rapidly after snowmelt or rain.

WHY IT MATTERS: A system must hold enough water without causing erosion.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBCreate a stable, protected growing space that holds water without depending on steep natural soil.
🌿
Brillig · Rainspout Tree

Heavy rain, drainage problems and nutrient loss if forest is cleared

1WATERRain can keep ground wet for long periods.

WHY IT MATTERS: Roots need drainage so the growing area does not stay waterlogged.

2SOILFrequent rain can wash dissolved materials through exposed soil.

WHY IT MATTERS: Growing media should hold useful materials while still draining.

3ECOSYSTEMRemoving forest cover increases erosion and damages habitat.

WHY IT MATTERS: A system that uses existing clearings or contained beds may reduce clearing.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBDrain excess water while keeping soil/materials in place and avoiding unnecessary forest clearing.
🏝️
Manxome · Storm Palm

Salt exposure, storms and vulnerable young plants

1SALTStorm spray can leave salt on soil and growing surfaces.

WHY IT MATTERS: Fresh-water control and drainage may be needed to prevent salt buildup.

2STORMSSevere storms can damage seedlings before roots are established.

WHY IT MATTERS: Young plants may need temporary shelter or protected growing.

3SOILIsland soils vary from sandy shore deposits to deeper inland material.

WHY IT MATTERS: A contained medium gives humans more control over drainage and rooting.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBProtect young plants from wind and salt while keeping fresh water moving through the root zone.
🏜️
Slithy Toves · Reservoir Thorn

Extreme water scarcity and the risk of salt buildup from irrigation

1WATERFresh water is the continent’s main limiting resource.

WHY IT MATTERS: A growing system must reduce wasted water.

2EVAPORATIONHot, dry air removes water quickly from exposed soil.

WHY IT MATTERS: Covering soil or delivering water slowly may reduce loss.

3SALTRepeated watering followed by evaporation can leave salts behind.

WHY IT MATTERS: More irrigation is not automatically a better solution.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBDeliver small amounts of water efficiently while reducing evaporation and avoiding salt buildup.
❄️
Wabe · Ember Moss

A very short growing season and persistently cold conditions

1TEMPERATURESurface soil is usable for only a short summer window.

WHY IT MATTERS: Protected growing could warm the root zone earlier and longer.

2LIGHTSummer days are long once the season begins.

WHY IT MATTERS: Light may be available even when temperature is the main limit.

3GROUNDFrozen or saturated ground can limit normal root-zone conditions.

WHY IT MATTERS: Raised or contained growing may separate plants from unsuitable ground.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBCreate a small protected growing zone that warms the root area and uses the long summer daylight.
🌾
Bandersnatch · Goldstem Grain

Excellent growing potential, but soil and water can be damaged by large-scale farming

1SOILPrairie soils can support strong plant growth.

WHY IT MATTERS: Humans should protect the productive top layer instead of exhausting or compacting it.

2WINDOpen plains can experience strong winds and dry surface soil.

WHY IT MATTERS: Bare soil is more vulnerable to moisture loss and erosion.

3WATERWater is available but not unlimited during dry periods.

WHY IT MATTERS: Irrigation should be targeted rather than automatic.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBSupport strong growth while keeping soil covered, reducing erosion and avoiding unnecessary water use.
🌲
Gimble · Ironwood

Slow tree growth, forest competition and a long replacement time

1LIGHTYoung trees grow best where enough light reaches the forest floor.

WHY IT MATTERS: Planting location matters as much as soil or water.

2SOILForest soils contain organic material and hold moisture under litter.

WHY IT MATTERS: Removing all surface material could reduce useful soil conditions.

3TIMEIronwood grows and reproduces slowly.

WHY IT MATTERS: A system that destroys mature forest faster than replacement is not sustainable.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBCreate a nursery-style starting system for young Ironwood without assuming the natural forest should be cleared.
🪷
Mimsy · Floatroot

Saturated, nutrient-poor ground and the danger of draining wetlands

1WATERNatural ground stays saturated or shallowly flooded.

WHY IT MATTERS: Adding drainage could destroy the ecosystem function the plant depends on.

2NUTRIENTSWetland soils contain relatively few available nutrients.

WHY IT MATTERS: A contained growing medium could add materials without changing the whole wetland.

3ROOT ZONEFloatroot tolerates waterlogged conditions better than many Earth crops.

WHY IT MATTERS: The system should fit the plant rather than forcing dry-soil conditions.

YOUR SOLUTION'S JOBSupport roots and nutrients in a contained or raised system without draining the surrounding wetland.
CLASS 3

Growing System Challenge

MODEL OR DESIGN

Create one simple solution to one growing problem. Your teacher may have you build a cup-sized model from shared materials or make a labelled testable design. It does not need to grow a plant today.

Drainage

holes, gravel or a medium that lets extra water leave

Water saving

wick/reservoir or cover that slows evaporation

Root-zone control

raised/contained medium separated from unsuitable ground

Protection

simple clear cover or shelter from cold/wind

  1. Choose the single growing problem your solution will solve.
  2. Choose only the shared materials or design features you actually need.
  3. Build one small model in a reused container or draw and label a testable design.
  4. If you built it, test one feature once. If you drew it, explain the one test you would use.
  5. Identify one problem your solution could create and one change that could reduce it.
Keep it simple.The science reasoning matters more than constructing a polished device.
