CLASS 1

Learn the Science

Plants of the same kind are not all identical. Humans can choose which differences they want to encourage.

🌼REPRODUCTION

Seed plants make new generations through reproduction. Pollination helps flowering plants form seeds. Some plants can also be propagated from cuttings, runners or other plant parts.

🌿PLANT VARIETY

Plants of the same kind can differ in height, yield, drought tolerance, strength, timing and resistance to pests.

🎯SELECTIVE BREEDING

Humans repeatedly choose plants with useful characteristics to reproduce so those characteristics become more common in cultivated varieties.

⚠️MONOCULTURE / LOW VARIETY

Large areas of very similar plants can be efficient, but one pest, disease or environmental change may affect many plants in the same way.

Grade 7 idea:People choose plants with characteristics they want and use those plants to produce future generations.
Need Help? What selective breeding is NOT in this mission

You do not need Punnett squares, alleles, genotype, phenotype or dominant/recessive inheritance here. Focus on observable plant characteristics, repeated human selection and the consequences of that choice.

Gyre · Skyroot

Short seasons favour cold-tolerant plants, but strong anchoring still matters on steep ground.

1SEASONSnow can remain late and the warm growing window is brief.

WHY IT MATTERS: Cold tolerance or short-season growth could help plants finish their life cycle.

2STABILITYSkyroot roots help hold thin soil on windy slopes.

WHY IT MATTERS: Selecting only for fast growth could reduce a feature that keeps plants anchored.

3TRADE-OFFThe fastest-growing trial plants had shallower roots.

WHY IT MATTERS: More growth is useful only if the plants still survive mountain conditions.

⚠️ Reveal the surprise event
An unusually cold, windy summer arrives two weeks earlier than expected.

Cold tolerance, short-season growth and strong anchoring would all help.

Ask: Did our selected population keep enough useful variety to handle this change?

AFTER THE SURPRISE

If a pest appears, humans have choices

KEEP IT SIMPLE
Biological control

Use a living organism or natural enemy to reduce the pest. It must be chosen carefully so it does not create a new problem.

Physical / cultural control

Use barriers, remove infected material, change planting patterns or use other management practices.

Chemical control

Use a pesticide or herbicide when appropriate. It may work quickly, but misuse can affect non-target organisms, soil or water.

Selective breeding can be useful.Variety and safeguards still matter.
