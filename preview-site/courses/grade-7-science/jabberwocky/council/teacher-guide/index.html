FINAL CAPSTONE // TEACHER IMPLEMENTATION

MISSION 2190
COUNCIL GUIDE

The Council is a 3-class year-long transfer task, not a sixth science unit. Students stay with their Phase 5 teams and continents and use exactly five concise Evidence Vault cards instead of reopening old Team Records.

CORE DESIGNEvidence Vault → Five-Lens Review → Compare Two Outcomes → Council Directive → ReflectionNo new content unit · essentially no-purchase
STUDENT ROLEJCEC Council Delegation
TEAM RECORD1 Mission 2190 Council Directive
INDIVIDUAL1 short reflection
TIME3 × 45 minutes

NON-NEGOTIABLE COUNCIL GUARDRAILS

NO ARCHIVE REOPENINGThe five Evidence Vault cards are the capstone evidence source.
NO PREDETERMINED OUTCOMEDo not score a continent against a hidden “correct” settlement status.
NO NEW JABBERWOCKY CANONDo not invent hazards, resources, energy systems, structures, fossil beds, faults or ecosystem conditions to make the decision easier.
NO VOCABULARY TESTStudents should apply science rather than re-memorize five units.
UNCERTAINTY COUNTSA responsible Council may limit or delay activity because evidence remains incomplete.
PROTECTION IS PART OF THE DECISIONSafeguards are not an optional add-on after the human-presence choice.

3-CLASS IMPLEMENTATION

What to do each day

CLASS 1

Open the Evidence Vault

BEFORE CLASS

Confirm each team keeps its Phase 5 team and continent. Open the Council page and verify the saved Phase 5 posting loads the correct five-card Evidence Vault. Decide whether students will work on paper, Chromebook or both.

MATERIALS

Mission 2190 Evidence Vault, Five-Lens Council Review, pencils and/or Chromebooks. No old Team Records.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Read exactly five evidence cards → sort what each lens supports, limits, protects and leaves uncertain → complete the Five-Lens Council Review → identify two plausible Council outcomes.

KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS

Cross-unit transfer: ecosystems, plant resources, thermal survival, structures/forces and geology all constrain one decision. Evidence is not the same as a recommendation.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Do not let students choose an outcome first and then hunt for supporting evidence. Every team must consider all five lenses. No continent has a predetermined correct answer.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Completed Five-Lens Council Review and two plausible candidate outcomes — capstone evidence in progress.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

If time is short, complete one strong SUPPORTS/LIMITS/PROTECTS/UNCERTAIN entry per lens and identify two candidate outcomes. Do not reopen archives.

CLASS 2

The Council Deliberates

BEFORE CLASS

Prepare the five outcome cards and the two-option comparison board. Review which safeguards are already established canon for each continent.

MATERIALS

Five-Lens Council Review, outcome cards, two-option comparison board, pencils/Chromebooks.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Compare at least two plausible outcomes → identify strongest supporting evidence, biggest limit, required safeguard and important uncertainty for each → discuss mixed directives where appropriate → choose a draft directive.

KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS

Scientific decision making weighs multiple forms of evidence, trade-offs, protection needs and uncertainty. A responsible recommendation can remain conditional.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

A directive is not a popularity vote. Environmental safeguards matter as much as human convenience. “Delay and collect more evidence” is a legitimate outcome when a critical uncertainty remains.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Two-option comparison and draft directive — year-long transfer reasoning.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Compare exactly two outcomes and require one explicit trade-off, one safeguard and one uncertainty before choosing.

CLASS 3

Issue the Mission 2190 Directive

BEFORE CLASS

Prepare one Mission 2190 Council Directive per team and one reflection prompt per student. Decide whether to use optional 60-second Council Statements or a quiet gallery after core work is complete.

MATERIALS

Council Directive, five evidence cards, completed review board, pencils/Chromebooks.

STUDENTS SEE / DO

Finalize level of human presence + where/scale + protection requirements → cite all five science lenses → select three non-negotiable safeguards → name one uncertainty and next investigation → write 4–6 sentence Council message → complete individual reflection.

KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS

A final scientific recommendation should be evidence-based, revisable and explicit about uncertainty, safeguards and next questions.

TEACHER EMPHASIS

Assess evidence integration and reasoning, not presentation polish or memorized vocabulary. One directive per team; one reflection per student.

COLLECT / ASSESS

Mission 2190 Council Directive + individual reflection — final year-long transfer task.

IF TIME RUNS OUT

Protect the directive, three safeguards, one uncertainty, five evidence lenses and individual reflection. Cut gallery/presentation first.

MISSION 2190 EVIDENCE VAULT

Exactly five concise science lenses

1ECOSYSTEM EVIDENCE + ECOLOGICAL SAFEGUARDPhase 1
2PLANT RESOURCE EVIDENCE + SUSTAINABILITY RULEPhase 2
3THERMAL EVIDENCE + SURVIVAL / ENERGY RULEPhase 3
4STRUCTURAL EVIDENCE + SAFETY / SITE RULEPhase 4
5GEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE + LIMIT / UNCERTAINTY / PROTECTION RULEPhase 5

Teacher check: the Evidence Vault summarizes established project canon. It must not become an answer key. The cards contain evidence and constraints only; students choose the final directive.

ASSESSMENT ROLE

Year-long transfer task

The Council is best assessed as evidence transfer and scientific decision making. It should not replace unit-specific science assessments and should not become a vocabulary exam.

EVIDENCE INTEGRATIONUses all five science lenses accurately and relevantly.
REASONING / TRADE-OFFSExplains why the directive fits the combined evidence better than at least one alternative.
SAFEGUARDS + UNCERTAINTYProtection rules are specific and uncertainty meaningfully affects the plan.
FINAL COMMUNICATIONCouncil message is concise, evidence-based and revisable if new evidence appears.

If a numeric score is needed, use four roughly equal criteria. Do not add points for artistic design, elaborate presentation or recalling old Team Record wording.

2-CLASS COMPRESSED FALLBACK

If the end-of-year calendar collapses

CLASS 1Evidence Vault + Review + Compare Two Outcomes

Read the five cards, complete one strong SUPPORTS/LIMITS/PROTECTS/UNCERTAIN entry per lens, compare exactly two outcomes, and select a draft directive.

CLASS 2Directive + Reflection + Closure

Finalize human-presence level, scale, three safeguards, protection, uncertainty, next investigation, Council message and individual reflection.

Cut gallery walks and presentations first. Do not cut the five-lens evidence review, uncertainty, safeguards or individual reflection.

COMMON END-OF-YEAR CONTINGENCIES

Keep the reasoning, not the ceremony

Phase 5 posting missingUse the teacher-assigned Phase 5 continent and restore it through the Phase 5 hub. Do not assign a new continent in the Council.
Students want old assignmentsRedirect to the five Evidence Vault cards. The capstone is intentionally archive-light.
Students pick an answer immediatelyRequire the Five-Lens Review and comparison of two plausible outcomes before a directive is accepted.
Students ignore environmental safeguardsRemind them that three non-negotiable safeguards and a protection statement are required parts of the final record.
Students think “delay” means failureReframe it as a scientific decision when a critical uncertainty remains.
Teams disagreeAsk which claim is better supported by the Evidence Vault; allow a mixed directive if the evidence supports it.
A class is lostSwitch to the approved two-class compressed pathway.
Printing/device failureProject the five evidence cards and use ordinary lined paper for the review/directive. No special template is required.

YEAR-LONG STORY CLOSURE

End with evidence—not conquest.

Avoid treating permanent settlement as the “successful” ending. The project is complete when students can make a defensible, revisable Council directive from multiple scientific constraints. Some teams may support long-term presence, some seasonal or research-only presence, some protection, and some delay for more evidence.

“Jabberwocky was never a puzzle with one correct answer. Science gave JCEC stronger evidence, clearer limits, better questions and reasons to protect systems that cannot simply be replaced.”
