LESSON 3.1 · GRADE 6 MATH

Prime Factorization & Divisibility

Compose products in different ways, break composite numbers into prime factors, and use factor structure to reason about common factors and divisibility.

START HERE

The big idea

Composite numbers can be broken into prime factors, and those prime factors reveal how the number is built.

A factor divides a number with no remainder. Prime numbers cannot be broken into smaller natural-number factors other than 1 and themselves. Composite numbers can. When we continue factoring until every factor is prime, we get a prime factorization. Different factor trees may look different, but they end with the same prime factors. Those factors help us reason about common factors and divisibility.

Think about itIf 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5, what can you tell immediately about numbers that divide 60 evenly?
📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

01

Factors build products

A factor is a natural number, such as 1, 2, 3, …, that divides another natural number with a remainder of 0. For 24, factor pairs include 1 × 24, 2 × 12, 3 × 8, and 4 × 6.

Remember: If a number divides exactly, it is a factor.

02

A product can be composed in multiple ways

The same natural number can be built from different factor groupings. For example, 72 = 8 × 9 = 6 × 12 = 2 × 4 × 3 × 3. Prime factorization keeps decomposing until every factor is prime.

Remember: Different products can have the same value; prime factorization shows the number’s prime building blocks.

03

Prime and composite describe factor structure

A prime number greater than 1 has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. A composite number greater than 1 has more than two factors. Zero and one are neither prime nor composite.

Remember: 2 is the only even prime number.

04

Factor trees uncover prime factors

Start with any factor pair for a composite number. If a factor is composite, split it again. Continue until every branch ends in a prime number. The prime numbers at the ends are the prime factors.

Remember: A factor tree is complete only when every final factor is prime.

05

Prime factorization is a number’s building plan

Prime factorization writes a composite number as a product of only prime numbers. For example, 60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5. Multiplying the prime factors recreates the original number.

Remember: Different factor trees for the same number lead to the same prime factorization, although the order may differ.

06

Prime factors reveal divisibility and common factors

If the factors needed to build one number are contained in the prime factorization of another, the division is exact. Shared prime factors also help identify factors that two numbers have in common.

Remember: Use the structure of the numbers as evidence instead of guessing from size.

COMMON MIX-UPS

“1 is prime because only 1 divides it.”

A prime number must have exactly two factors: 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.

“A factor tree must start with a particular factor pair.”

You may begin with any correct factor pair. A complete tree will still end with the same collection of prime factors.

“If a number is large, it must be composite.”

Size does not determine whether a number is prime. For example, 97 is prime because its only natural-number factors are 1 and 97.

“If two numbers share one prime factor, that is their only common factor.”

Shared prime factors can combine to make larger common factors. For example, 12 and 18 share a factor of 2 and a factor of 3, so they also share 2 × 3 = 6.

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Prime or composite?

Is 29 prime or composite?

  1. Check possible factor pairs.
  2. 29 is not divisible evenly by 2, 3, 4, or 5.
  3. The only factors are 1 and 29.
Answer: 29 is prime.

Example 2 · Prime factorization

Find the prime factorization of 72.

  1. Start with 72 = 8 × 9.
  2. Factor 8 as 2 × 2 × 2 and 9 as 3 × 3.
  3. All final factors are prime.
  4. Check: 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 72.
Answer: 72 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3.

Example 3 · Divisibility from factors

Is 84 divisible by 14?

  1. Prime factorize 84: 84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7.
  2. Prime factorize 14: 14 = 2 × 7.
  3. The factors 2 and 7 needed to build 14 are both contained in 84.
  4. 84 ÷ 14 therefore has remainder 0.
Answer: Yes. 84 ÷ 14 = 6.

Example 4 · Common factor

Use prime factors to find a common factor greater than 1 of 30 and 48.

  1. 30 = 2 × 3 × 5.
  2. 48 = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3.
  3. Both contain a factor 2 and a factor 3.
  4. 2 × 3 = 6, so 6 is a common factor.
Answer: 6 is a common factor of 30 and 48.

OFF-SCREEN ACTIVITY

Factor Card Investigation

  1. Choose a composite number from 20 to 100.
  2. Write one factor pair on two cards or scraps of paper.
  3. Keep splitting any composite factor until every card shows a prime number.
  4. Rearrange the prime cards and multiply them to check that they rebuild the starting number.
  5. Try a different first factor pair and compare the final prime factors.

Reflect: What stayed the same when you started the factor tree in a different way?

Working on your own? Use small scraps of paper for factor branches. Keep splitting composite factors until every final card is prime.

PLAY & APPLY

Shared Factor Packing Challenge

REAL-LIFE CHALLENGE

A class has 84 red tiles and 126 blue tiles. The tiles must be split into the same number of equal packs, with no tiles left over.

  1. Use the prime factorizations of 84 and 126 to identify the prime factors they share.
  2. Build a common factor from those shared prime factors.
  3. Use that common factor as the number of packs. How many red tiles and blue tiles go in each pack?
  4. Explain why your number of packs works for both colours.
Check the core ideas

84 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 and 126 = 2 × 3 × 3 × 7. They share 2 × 3 × 7 = 42, so 42 packs work. Each pack gets 2 red tiles and 3 blue tiles. The goal is to reason from shared prime factors, not memorize a separate greatest-common-factor procedure.

WRAP UP

LESSON SUMMARY

  • Factors divide exactly with remainder 0.
  • Prime numbers have exactly two factors; composite numbers have more than two.
  • Prime factorization breaks a composite number into prime factors only.
  • Different correct factor trees lead to the same prime factors.
  • Prime factors help explain common factors and divisibility.
  • The same product can be composed in multiple ways before it is decomposed into prime factors.

IF YOU NEED SUPPORT

  • List factor pairs before deciding whether a number is prime or composite.
  • Circle every final prime factor on a factor tree.
  • Multiply your prime factors to check the original number.
  • For divisibility, prime factorize both numbers and compare what factors are needed.

IF YOU WANT A CHALLENGE

  • Find two different factor trees for 180 and show why they produce the same prime factorization.
  • Use prime factors to find all common factors of 72 and 90.
  • Explain a method for finding the greatest common factor using prime factorizations.

PAT-STYLE THINKING

A student says 66 is prime because it is not in the 5-times table. Which response best explains the error? A) Every even number greater than 2 is composite. B) Prime numbers must end in 5. C) 66 is prime because 6 + 6 = 12. D) Only multiples of 5 can be composite.

Show answer

A. Since 66 is even and greater than 2, it has at least the factors 1, 2, 33, and 66, so it is composite.
