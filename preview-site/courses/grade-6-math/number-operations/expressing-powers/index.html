Expressing Powers

Represent repeated multiplication with powers and connect powers to prime factorization and divisibility.

START HERE

The big idea

A power is a compact way to communicate repeated multiplication of the same factor.

In a power such as 4³, the base 4 is the repeated factor and the exponent 3 tells how many copies of 4 are multiplied. The exponent does not mean multiply the base by the exponent. Power notation is especially useful when the same prime factor appears many times in a prime factorization.

Think about itWhy does 2⁵ mean 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 rather than 2 × 5?
📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

01

Powers communicate repeated multiplication

When the same factor appears again and again, power notation gives a shorter representation. For example, 7 × 7 × 7 × 7 can be written as 7⁴.

Remember: A product can become one power only when the repeated factors are identical.

02

The base is the repeated factor

In 5³, the base is 5. It tells us which number is being multiplied repeatedly.

Remember: The base stays full-sized and sits on the main line.

03

The exponent counts the factors

In 5³, the exponent is 3. It tells us there are three factors of 5: 5 × 5 × 5.

Remember: The exponent counts copies of the base; it is not another factor to multiply by the base.

04

Prime factorizations can be compressed with powers

A prime factorization such as 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 can be written 2³ × 3². This keeps the same factors but communicates them more efficiently.

Remember: Group identical prime factors before writing powers.

05

A power is divisible by its base

Because a power contains repeated copies of its base as factors, one copy of the base can always divide the product exactly. For example, 3⁴ = 3 × 3 × 3 × 3, so it is divisible by 3.

Remember: Reason from the repeated factors, not from a memorized rule.

COMMON MIX-UPS

“4³ means 4 × 3.”

The exponent tells how many 4s are factors. 4³ = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64, not 12.

“3 × 3 × 4 can be written as 3³.”

A single power represents repeated identical factors only. Here only the two 3s repeat, so the expression could be written 3² × 4.

“The base and exponent can be swapped.”

They have different jobs. 2³ = 8 while 3² = 9, so changing their positions changes the value.

“2³ × 3² means all five factors are the same.”

It represents two groups of repeated factors: three 2s and two 3s.

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Read a power

Identify the base and exponent in 6⁴.

  1. The full-sized repeated factor is 6, so 6 is the base.
  2. The small raised number is 4, so 4 is the exponent.
  3. The exponent tells us there are four factors of 6.
Answer: Base = 6; exponent = 4.

Example 2 · Expand a power

Write 3⁵ as repeated multiplication.

  1. The base is 3.
  2. The exponent is 5, so write five factors of 3.
Answer: 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 3.

Example 3 · Compress repeated factors

Write 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 5 × 5 using powers where possible.

  1. There are four identical factors of 2, so write 2⁴.
  2. There are two identical factors of 5, so write 5².
  3. Keep the multiplication between the two groups.
Answer: 2⁴ × 5².

Example 4 · Divisibility

Explain why 7⁴ is divisible by 7 without evaluating 7⁴.

  1. Expand the meaning: 7⁴ = 7 × 7 × 7 × 7.
  2. One factor of 7 can be separated from the product.
  3. The remaining product is still a natural number.
Answer: 7⁴ is divisible by 7 because 7 is one of its factors.

Code Compression Challenge

REAL-LIFE CHALLENGE

A game stores repeated multiplication in compact notation. One score rule contains 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3, and another contains 5 × 5 × 5.

  1. Rewrite each rule using powers.
  2. Identify the base and exponent of every power you wrote.
  3. Evaluate each rule.
  4. Explain why the first score is divisible by both 2 and 3 without using long division.
Check the core ideas

The first rule is 2⁴ × 3² = 16 × 9 = 144. The second is 5³ = 125. The first score contains factors of both 2 and 3, so it is divisible by each.

LESSON SUMMARY

  • A power represents repeated multiplication of identical factors.
  • The base is the repeated factor.
  • The exponent tells how many copies of the base are factors.
  • Repeated prime factors can be compressed using powers.
  • A power is divisible by its base because the base is one of its factors.

IF YOU NEED SUPPORT

  • Circle the base and write the exponent as a count before expanding.
  • Write repeated factors one at a time and count them carefully.
  • Group identical prime factors before converting a prime factorization into powers.

IF YOU WANT A CHALLENGE

  • Compare 2⁶ and 4³ by expanding both. What do you notice?
  • Find three different powers whose values are less than 200.
  • Write 360 as a prime factorization and then compress repeated factors using powers.

PAT-STYLE THINKING

Which expression has the same value as 3⁴? A) 3 × 4 B) 4 × 4 × 4 C) 3 × 3 × 3 × 3 D) 3 + 3 + 3 + 3
