Reflecting Shapes in the Cartesian Plane

Create and describe mirror images across lines of reflection.

START HERE

The big idea

A reflection flips a figure across a mirror line so matching points stay the same perpendicular distance from that line.

Across the y-axis, the x-coordinate changes sign while y stays the same. Across the x-axis, y changes sign while x stays the same. A reflection preserves size and shape but reverses orientation.

Think about itWhy does a point on the line of reflection stay fixed?
📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

01

A reflection is a flip

The line of reflection acts like a mirror. The original and image lie on opposite sides.

Remember: Reflect across the named axis, not toward it.

02

Measure perpendicular distance

Join a point to its image with the shortest path to the mirror line. Both points are equally far from that line.

Remember: Count spaces straight across the axis.

03

Across y, x changes

Reflecting (x, y) across the y-axis gives (−x, y).

Remember: The y-axis is vertical, so the left-right x-location reverses.

04

Across x, y changes

Reflecting (x, y) across the x-axis gives (x, −y).

Remember: The x-axis is horizontal, so the up-down y-location reverses.

COMMON MIX-UPS

“Across the x-axis means change x.”

The x-axis is the mirror line. The vertical y-location reverses.

“A reflection is a slide.”

A reflection reverses orientation; a translation does not.

“Every coordinate changes sign.”

Only the coordinate perpendicular to the mirror axis changes sign.

🧩

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Across the y-axis

Reflect P(−6, 3) across the y-axis.

  1. The y-axis is the mirror line.
  2. Reverse the left-right x-location.
  3. Keep y = 3.
Answer: P′(6, 3)

Example 2 · Across the x-axis

Reflect Q(4, −2) across the x-axis.

  1. Keep x = 4.
  2. Reverse the up-down y-location.
  3. Check equal distance from the x-axis.
Answer: Q′(4, 2)

Example 3 · Point on an axis

Reflect R(0, 5) across the y-axis.

  1. R lies on the y-axis.
  2. Its perpendicular distance from the mirror line is 0.
  3. It therefore maps onto itself.
Answer: R′(0, 5)

OFF-SCREEN ACTIVITY

Mirror-Line Test

  1. Draw a polygon that does not cross an axis.
  2. Choose the x-axis or y-axis as the mirror line.
  3. Use tracing paper or careful counting to reflect every vertex.
  4. Draw segments from each point to its image.
  5. Check that each segment crosses the mirror line at a right angle and is split in half.

Reflect: What evidence proves that your image is a reflection rather than a translation?

Working on your own? Fold tracing paper along the chosen axis, or count equal spaces on both sides of the mirror line.

PLAY & APPLY

Logo-Mirror Challenge

REAL-LIFE CHALLENGE

A triangular logo has vertices A(2, 1), B(7, 1), and C(5, 5). It must be mirrored across the y-axis.

  1. Find A′, B′, and C′.
  2. Describe what stayed the same.
  3. Describe what changed.
  4. Explain how you could verify the mirror line.
Check the core ideas

A′(−2, 1), B′(−7, 1), C′(−5, 5). The y-values stay; each x-value changes sign. The y-axis is halfway between matching points.

WRAP UP

Ready to move on?

LESSON SUMMARY

  • A reflection is a flip.
  • Matching points are equally far from the mirror line.
  • Across y, x changes sign.
  • Across x, y changes sign.
  • Reflections preserve size and shape but reverse orientation.

IF YOU NEED SUPPORT

  • Trace the original shape and image in two colours.
  • Work one vertex at a time and label each image point with a prime.
  • Use grid paper or the on-page model whenever you need a visual.

IF YOU WANT A CHALLENGE

  • Reflect a shape across both axes and describe the combined result.
  • Design a polygon with one vertex that stays fixed during reflection.
  • Find the mirror line from a point and its image.

PAT-STYLE THINKING

M(−4, −7) is reflected across the x-axis. Which image is correct? A) (4, −7) B) (−4, 7) C) (4, 7) D) (−7, −4)

Show answer

B. Across the x-axis, x stays −4 and y changes sign.
