LESSON 2.6 · GRADE 6 MATH

Tessellations

Create repeating designs that cover a surface without gaps or overlaps.

START HERE

The big idea

A tessellation repeats one or more tiles to cover a surface completely with no gaps and no overlaps.

Translations, reflections, and rotations can move congruent tiles into a repeating design. A tile does not need to be symmetrical by itself. The symmetry of the finished tessellation can differ from the symmetry of its individual tile.

Think about itWhy can congruent circles not cover a flat surface without gaps or overlaps?
📘

LEARN

Build your understanding

01

Two conditions must be true

A tessellation covers the surface with no uncovered gaps and no overlapping tiles.

Remember: Repeating is not enough: check coverage.

02

Transformations build the pattern

Tiles may slide, flip, or turn into their repeated positions.

Remember: Name the movement from one tile to a matching tile.

03

The tile and pattern can have different symmetry

An asymmetrical tile can be arranged into a symmetrical tessellation, and a symmetrical tile can form a pattern with fewer symmetries.

Remember: Analyze the tile and the whole design separately.

04

Tessellations appear in design

Artists, architects, builders, and many cultural traditions use repeating geometric designs. Study each example in its own context and avoid assuming a design’s meaning.

Remember: Describe the mathematics while respecting the source and story of the design.

COMMON MIX-UPS

“Any repeating pattern is a tessellation.”

The shapes must also cover the surface with no gaps or overlaps.

“Only regular polygons tessellate.”

Many irregular or asymmetrical tiles can tessellate.

“The tile and pattern have identical symmetry.”

The arrangement can add or remove symmetry compared with one tile.

🧩

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Example 1 · Square grid

Do congruent squares arranged edge-to-edge tessellate?

  1. The pattern repeats.
  2. Every edge meets another edge.
  3. There are no gaps.
  4. There are no overlaps.
Answer: Yes, the square grid is a tessellation.

Example 2 · Circle pattern

Do congruent circles in rows tessellate?

  1. The circles can repeat.
  2. Curved boundaries leave spaces between neighbouring circles.
  3. Those uncovered spaces are gaps.
Answer: No, congruent circles alone do not tessellate the plane.

Example 3 · Describe movement

A tile repeats in a straight row without turning.

  1. Choose matching points on neighbouring tiles.
  2. Notice each point moves the same distance and direction.
  3. Name that transformation.
Answer: The row is generated by a translation.

CURRICULUM INVESTIGATION

Find tessellations in objects, art, and architecture

2.6 focus

Study each design as evidence. Ask: Does it repeat? Are there gaps? Are there overlaps? Which transformations move one part of the pattern onto another?

Choose a design.Describe the evidence before opening the explanation.
Studying culturally inspired geometric designs

First Nations, Métis, and Inuit designs can contain rich symmetry and repeating geometry. Use a reliable source that names the community, artist, or tradition when possible. Describe the mathematics you can observe, and learn the design’s context rather than assuming that every pattern has the same meaning.

OFF-SCREEN ACTIVITY

Create a Paper Tessellation

  1. Start with a small square paper tile.
  2. Cut a shape from one side and tape it to the opposite side without turning it.
  3. Trace copies so each tile fits against the next.
  4. Cover a rectangular region with no gaps or overlaps.
  5. Colour a repeating unit and mark any lines or centres of symmetry.

Reflect: Which transformations move one copy of your tile onto another?

Working on your own? Cut one paper tile and trace it repeatedly; the on-page builder also works without extra materials.

REAL-LIFE CHALLENGE

Create a repeating tile design for a walkway, wall, or digital background.

  1. Choose or create a tile.
  2. Show at least 12 copies with no gaps or overlaps.
  3. Mark one repeating unit.
  4. Name the transformations that build the pattern.
  5. Describe any reflectional or rotational symmetry.
  6. If inspired by a cultural design, name and learn from a reliable source rather than copying without context.
Check the core ideas

Solutions vary. A successful design fully covers the chosen region, identifies its repeating unit, and accurately describes transformations and symmetry.

LESSON SUMMARY

  • A tessellation repeats to cover a surface.
  • It has no gaps and no overlaps.
  • Transformations position the tiles.
  • A tile and its full pattern can have different symmetry.
  • Design examples should be studied with respect for their cultural context.

IF YOU NEED SUPPORT

  • Trace the original shape and image in two colours.
  • Work one vertex at a time and label each image point with a prime.
  • Use grid paper or the on-page model whenever you need a visual.

IF YOU WANT A CHALLENGE

  • Test which regular polygons tessellate by themselves.
  • Create an asymmetrical tile that makes a symmetrical pattern.
  • Combine two tile types into one repeating unit.

PAT-STYLE THINKING

A design repeats congruent shapes but leaves small uncovered spaces. Which statement is correct? A) It is a tessellation because it repeats. B) It is not a tessellation because it has gaps. C) It is congruent, so gaps do not matter. D) It becomes a tessellation after colouring.

Show answer

B. A tessellation must have no gaps and no overlaps.
