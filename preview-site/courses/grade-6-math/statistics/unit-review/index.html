PAT-STYLE REASONING

Explain what the data really says

9.1

A fair coin gives 7 heads in 10 tosses. Does that prove heads is more likely?

Strong answer

No. Fairness describes equal chance on each toss. A small random sample can be uneven, so 7 heads does not prove the underlying chances changed.

9.2

One class has 15 of 30 choose basketball. Another has 24 of 48. Which class has the greater relative frequency?

Strong answer

Neither. 15/30=50% and 24/48=50%. The raw counts differ, but the part-to-whole relationships are the same.

9.3

Why might 51 heads in 100 tosses be stronger evidence for a fair coin than 9 heads in 10 tosses?

Strong answer

The 100-toss sample contains much more data and gives 51%, close to the expected 50%. The 10-toss sample is more easily affected by short-run random variation.

WHOLE-UNIT SYNTHESIS

A four-colour spinner is designed with equal sections.

  1. List what makes the four outcomes equally likely.
  2. A student spins 20 times and gets red 8 times. Find red's relative frequency.
  3. Another student spins 200 times and gets red 52 times. Find red's relative frequency.
  4. Compare both results with the expected 25%.
  5. Which sample should usually support the stronger estimate, and why?
Show a strong solution

Equal sections give each colour the same chance. The first sample gives 8/20=40%. The second gives 52/200=26%. Both can occur in a fair random experiment, but the 200-spin sample is much larger and its 26% is close to the expected 25%, so it usually supports the stronger estimate.
