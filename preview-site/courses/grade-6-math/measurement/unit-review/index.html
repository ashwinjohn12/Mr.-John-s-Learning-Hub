PAT-STYLE THINKING

Explain, justify, and connect

7.1

Why slant does not control area

Two parallelograms have the same base and perpendicular height but different slants. Explain why their areas are equal.

Show a strong answer

Each can be rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. The slant changes the shape, but not the amount of space covered.

7.2

Why the triangle gets a half

Explain why triangle area uses base × height ÷ 2.

Show a strong answer

A matching copy of the triangle can form a parallelogram with the same base and height. The two congruent triangles share that parallelogram equally, so one is half its area.

7.3

Different decompositions

Two students split a composite figure differently but get the same total. Explain how both can be correct.

Show a strong answer

If each set of non-overlapping pieces covers the original region exactly once and every component area is correct, the internal lines only organize the work; they do not change total area.

7.4

Why hidden cubes count

A student counts only cubes visible on the outside of a solid prism. Explain the mistake.

Show a strong answer

Volume measures all space occupied, including cubes inside and behind visible faces. Count a complete base layer and multiply by the number of layers.

SYNTHESIS · 7.1 + 7.2 + 7.3 + 7.4

Community Pavilion Design

A pavilion floor is a composite region made from a parallelogram and triangular entrance. Supplies are stored in rectangular-prism crates. Explain how the same measurement idea moves from rearranging area, to taking half, to decomposing, to stacking layers.

Show a strong answer

Parallelogram area comes from rearranging into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. Triangle area is half of a matching parallelogram. Composite area combines familiar component areas. Volume then extends the idea into three dimensions by repeating a base area through height.
