START HERE

The big idea

A parallelogram can be rearranged into a rectangle without changing its area.

When a triangular piece is cut from one side of a parallelogram and moved to the other side, the shape becomes a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height. The amount of space has not changed, so the area is base × perpendicular height. The slanted side does not tell how tall the shape is above its base.

1Choose the basePick a side as the reference.
2Find the 90° heightUse perpendicular height, not slant.
3MultiplyBase × perpendicular height.
Think about itIf two parallelograms have the same base and perpendicular height but different slants, should they have the same area?

LEARN

Build your understanding

01

Area measures space inside a 2-D region

Area describes how much two-dimensional space a shape covers. It is measured in square units such as cm², m², or in².

Remember: Perimeter is distance around; area is space inside.

02

Any side can be the base

A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel sides. You may choose any side as the base, but the matching height must be perpendicular to that base.

Remember: Base and height must meet at a right angle.

03

Perpendicular height is not slant height

The perpendicular height is the shortest distance from the base to the opposite side. It forms a 90° angle with the base. The slanted side is not used for area.

Remember: Look for the right-angle marker.

04

Rearranging keeps the same area

Cutting off a triangular end and moving it to the opposite side creates a rectangle. No area is added or removed.

Remember: Same pieces → same total area.

05

Multiply base × perpendicular height

Because the rearranged rectangle has the same base and perpendicular height, its area is found with the same multiplication: A = b × h.

Remember: Use square units in the final answer.

Missing dimensions and algebra

Use division for a missing dimension

If area and one dimension are known, use the inverse relationship. base = area ÷ height or height = area ÷ base.

Remember: This connects directly to solving equations in Unit 6.

COMMON MIX-UPS

“The slanted side is the height.”

Height must be perpendicular to the chosen base. A slanted side may be longer, but it does not measure the straight-up distance from base to opposite side.

“A more slanted parallelogram has more area.”

If base and perpendicular height stay the same, changing the slant does not change area.

“Area uses linear units.”

Area counts square units, so write cm², m², in², and so on.

“To find a missing height, subtract.”

Area is a product. Undo multiplication with division.

WATCH THE THINKING

Worked examples

Start with the two core examples. Open Examples 3 and 4 when you are ready for missing dimensions or richer applications.

Example 1 · Find area

A parallelogram has base 8 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. Find its area.

  1. Identify the base: 8 cm.
  2. Identify the perpendicular height: 5 cm.
  3. Multiply: 8 × 5 = 40.
  4. Write the area in square units.
Answer: 40 cm²

Example 2 · Ignore the slant height

A parallelogram has base 12 m, perpendicular height 7 m, and slanted side 9 m. Find the area.

  1. The slanted side is extra information for this calculation.
  2. Use base × perpendicular height.
  3. 12 × 7 = 84.
Answer: 84 m²
Ready for more? Open Examples 3 and 4

Example 3 · Find a missing base

Area = 64 in² and perpendicular height = 16 in. Find the base.

  1. Write the relationship: 64 = b × 16.
  2. Undo ×16 by dividing by 16.
  3. 64 ÷ 16 = 4.
Answer: 4 in

Example 4 · Real-world design

A parallelogram-shaped flower bed has area 90 ft² and base 8 ft. Find its perpendicular height.

  1. Write 90 = 8 × h.
  2. Divide: h = 90 ÷ 8.
  3. 90 ÷ 8 = 11.25.
Answer: 11.25 ft

LESSON SUMMARY

  • Parallelogram area can be understood by rearranging it into a rectangle.
  • Use the perpendicular height, not the slanted side.
  • Area = base × perpendicular height.
  • Use division to find a missing base or height.
  • Area answers use square units.
Need more help?
  • Highlight the base and draw the perpendicular height with a right-angle marker.
  • Rotate the diagram so the chosen base is horizontal.
  • Use grid paper and physically rearrange the shape.
  • Write A = b × h before substituting values.
Want an extra challenge?
  • Create three different parallelograms with area 48 cm².
  • Explain why a parallelogram can have a perpendicular height outside the shape.
  • Use coordinates to draw a parallelogram and determine its area.

PAT-STYLE THINKING

A student uses the 9 cm slanted side instead of the 6 cm perpendicular height with a 10 cm base. Explain the error.

Show a strong answer

Area measures base times the perpendicular distance to the opposite side. The slanted side does not meet the base at 90°, so the correct area is 10 × 6 = 60 cm².
