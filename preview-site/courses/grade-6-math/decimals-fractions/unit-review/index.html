PAT-STYLE THINKING

Explain, justify, and choose a strategy

4.1

Common denominators

A student solves 1/4 + 1/6 using twenty-fourths instead of twelfths and still gets 5/12 after simplifying. Is the strategy valid? Explain.

Show a strong answer

Yes. Twenty-fourths are a valid common unit: 1/4 = 6/24 and 1/6 = 4/24, so the sum is 10/24 = 5/12. Twelfths would be more efficient because 12 is the lowest common denominator, but the other strategy still preserves the values.

4.2

Multiplication meaning

Explain two different ways to interpret 4 × 2/5 and why the product does not require changing the denominator.

Show a strong answer

It can mean four groups of 2/5, so 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5 = 8/5. It can also be connected to taking repeated fifth-sized parts. The denominator stays 5 because the size of each fractional unit stays fifths.

4.3

Fraction as quotient

Five pizzas are shared equally among four families. Explain why 5 ÷ 4, 5/4, 1 1/4, and 1.25 all represent the same share.

Show a strong answer

Equal sharing is division, so each share is 5 ÷ 4. A fraction bar also means division, so 5 ÷ 4 = 5/4. The improper fraction is 1 1/4 as a mixed number, and one quarter equals 0.25, so the decimal is 1.25.

4.4

Decimal alignment

A student writes 5.8 + 0.47 with the final digits lined up instead of the decimal points. Explain the mathematical error without saying only “line up the decimals.”

Show a strong answer

The digits must be combined with digits representing the same place-value units. Ones belong with ones, tenths with tenths, and hundredths with hundredths. Aligning decimal points is the visible result of matching equal place-value units.

4.5

Decimal product

Without using a decimal-point shortcut, explain why 12.8 × 39 = 499.2 if 128 × 39 = 4992.

Show a strong answer

12.8 is 128 tenths. Multiplying 128 tenths by 39 gives 4992 tenths. Renaming 4992 tenths in decimal notation gives 499.2. An estimate near 13 × 40 = 520 also shows the result is reasonable.

4.6

Remainder in context

The calculation 15 ÷ 2 gives 7 R1. Explain why this could mean 7 bracelets with 1 charm left, 8 seats needed, or $7.50 per person.

Show a strong answer

The arithmetic does not change, but the context does. A partial bracelet may not count, so the extra charm stays left over. A person cannot be left without a seat, so another whole seat is needed. Money can be regrouped into smaller units, so the remainder can continue as 50 cents in each equal share.
