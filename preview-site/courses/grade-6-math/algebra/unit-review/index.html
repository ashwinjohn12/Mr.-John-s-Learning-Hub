PAT-STYLE THINKING

Explain, justify, and connect

6.1

Why unlike terms stay separate

A student says 4x + 3y = 7xy. Explain the error.

Show a strong answer

4x and 3y are unlike terms because their variable parts are different. Addition does not create a product xy. The expression is already simplified as 4x + 3y.

6.2

Why both sides must change

A student solves x + 5 = 12 by subtracting 5 only from the left side. Explain why the equation is no longer balanced.

Show a strong answer

The equal sign says both sides have the same value. Changing only one side destroys that equality. Subtract 5 from both sides to preserve equality and get x = 7.

6.3

Verification catches mistakes

A student claims x = 3 solves 2x + 5 = 12. Determine whether the claim is correct without first re-solving the equation.

Show a strong answer

Substitute 3 into the original equation. LHS = 2(3) + 5 = 11 while RHS = 12. Because LHS ≠ RHS, x = 3 is not a solution.

SYNTHESIS · 6.1 + 6.2 + 6.3

School Event Equation Challenge

Three student teams each collect the same number of cans, and 6 extra cans are donated. The total is 42. Write an equation, solve it, and verify the solution. Then explain where simplifying, balance thinking, and algebraic solving appear.

Show a strong answer

Let c be cans per team. The equation is 3c + 6 = 42. Subtract 6 from both sides to get 3c = 36, then divide both sides by 3 to get c = 12. Verify: 3(12) + 6 = 42. The expression is already simplified; the same operations on both sides preserve the balance; inverse operations isolate c; substitution verifies the solution.
