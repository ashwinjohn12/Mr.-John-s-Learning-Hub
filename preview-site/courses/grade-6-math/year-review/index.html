EXPLAIN IT

Nine reasoning prompts

Being able to explain why a method works is stronger than recognizing an answer. Try one prompt from each unit aloud or on paper before opening the model response.

Unit 1 · Integers

Explain why −3 is greater than −8 even though 8 has the greater magnitude.

Show a strong answer

Value and magnitude are different. −3 is farther right on the number line, so −3 > −8. The magnitude of −8 is greater because it is farther from zero.

Unit 2 · Geometry

A reflection and a translation both preserve size and shape. What important difference can you describe?

Show a strong answer

A translation slides every point the same distance and direction and keeps orientation. A reflection flips the figure across a mirror line and reverses orientation.

Unit 3 · Number structure

Why can 72 be written as 8 × 9 and also as 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 3?

Show a strong answer

A product can be composed in different ways. 8 × 9 and the prime factorization both equal 72; the prime factorization simply continues decomposing until every factor is prime.

Unit 4 · Fractions & decimals

Why is estimating useful even when you know how to use a standard algorithm?

Show a strong answer

An estimate predicts the size of the answer. After calculating exactly, you can compare with the estimate to catch place-value or operation errors.

Unit 5 · Proportional reasoning

Two stores sell different package sizes. Why is a unit rate often more useful than comparing the total prices?

Show a strong answer

A unit rate changes both offers to the same comparison unit, such as cost per item, so the different package sizes can be compared fairly.

Unit 6 · Algebra

Why must you perform an equivalent operation on both sides of an equation?

Show a strong answer

An equation says both sides have equal value. Doing the same valid operation to both sides preserves that equality while helping isolate the unknown.

Unit 7 · Measurement

Why does a triangle with base 10 cm and height 6 cm have half the area of a parallelogram with the same base and height?

Show a strong answer

Two congruent copies of the triangle can form a parallelogram with the same base and perpendicular height, so one triangle occupies half of that area.

Unit 8 · Patterns

How can a table, ordered pairs, a graph, and an equation represent the same function?

Show a strong answer

They encode the same input-output pairs in different forms. If they represent one function, an input such as x=3 must lead to the same output in every representation.

Unit 9 · Statistics

Why does 51 heads in 100 tosses usually provide stronger evidence about a fair coin than 8 heads in 10 tosses?

Show a strong answer

The larger sample contains more trials and is generally less affected by short-run random variation. It gives 51%, close to the expected 50%, but it still supports an estimate rather than proving a guarantee.
