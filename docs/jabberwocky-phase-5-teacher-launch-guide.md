# Jabberwocky: Mission 2190 — Phase 5 Teacher Launch Guide

## PHASE 5 — THE DEEP RECORD
**JCEC Geological Survey Division**

This teacher guide matches the completed student experience exactly:

1. **Mission 1 — Read the Ground — 3 classes — formative**
2. **Mission 2 — Follow the Rock Story — 3 classes — formative**
3. **Mission 3 — Track the Changing Surface — 4 classes — stronger geological-science reasoning checkpoint**
4. **Mission 4 — Read the Deep Past — 4 classes — formative synthesis**
5. **Mission 5 — Prepare the Final Evidence — 4 classes — major Phase 5 synthesis**

**18 core classes + 7 purposeful flex periods = 25 classes.**

> Low-material rule: **ordinary classroom supplies → shared/reused equipment → printables/data fallbacks → specialty equipment only as optional enrichment.**

> Phase boundary: **Mission 5 ends with a geological evidence handoff. Do not begin the final Mission 2190 Council decision yet.**

## Core pacing system

Every core class follows:

**BEFORE CLASS → MATERIALS → STUDENTS SEE / DO → KEY SCIENCE → TEACHER EMPHASIS → COLLECT / ASSESS → IF TIME RUNS OUT**

### Day 1 — Mission 1 — How Do Geologists Know?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open the Phase 5 Hub; assign/confirm Geological Survey continent; preload Earth Reference Evidence strips; decide whether physical samples or evidence cards will be used on Day 2.
- **MATERIALS:** Devices/projector; Phase 5 posting; Previous Team Geological Briefing; Earth-interior/tool visuals.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Phase launch; evidence-source distinction; observation/property/rock/mineral/classification; broad crust–mantle–core model; Earth-study tools; gradual/sudden-change preview.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Geology begins with evidence; scientists use direct and indirect evidence to classify materials and build models.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Evidence first, rock names later. Keep Earth examples separate from Jabberwocky canon.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Observation-vs-inference check — formative.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Keep the evidence-source rule + broad Earth model; cut extra examples.

### Day 2 — Mission 1 — Read the Samples
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Set 4–6 safe sample stations OR print/project photo/property cards; prepare relative-hardness references.
- **MATERIALS:** Existing school samples optional; trays; rulers; a few shared hand lenses; fingernail/copper/teacher-approved steel references; classification key.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** **OBSERVE → TEST → DESCRIBE → CLASSIFY → STATE UNCERTAINTY**.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Common terminology and repeatable property observations strengthen classification.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No destructive rock testing, no loose glass plates, no forced sample names.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Station evidence notes — formative.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Use three evidence cards instead of all stations.

### Day 3 — Mission 1 — What Does Our Ground Evidence Tell Us?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open the assigned continent’s Jabberwocky Survey Sample Packet; prepare Ground Evidence Card.
- **MATERIALS:** Device/projector; survey packet; Ground Evidence Card; pencils.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Separate observation from interpretation; choose strongest evidence; state uncertainty and one useful Earth-study tool.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Claims should be proportional to evidence.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Do not invent exact ages, faults, fossils, hazards or deposits.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** **Ground Evidence Card — formative.**
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Keep three useful observations/properties + uncertainty.

### Day 4 — Mission 2 — Three Ways Rocks Form
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare formation-process cards and one-clue-does-not-prove-it examples.
- **MATERIALS:** Igneous/sedimentary/metamorphic evidence cards; process cards.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Rock vs mineral recap; three rock classes through formation process; process-network rock cycle.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** **EVIDENCE → PROCESS → ROCK CLASS**.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Process first; loose sediment is not automatically sedimentary rock.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Process/class quick check — formative.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** One representative clue per rock class + the process network.

### Day 5 — Mission 2 — Follow the Rock Story
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare 4–5 Rock Cycle Evidence Lab cases; cache the Calgary-area Alberta Geological Survey evidence card.
- **MATERIALS:** Existing rock samples optional; photos; process/evidence/rock-class cards; hand lenses optional.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Evidence → process → rock class → uncertainty; short local Alberta evidence connection.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Several observations can support a formation history, but uncertainty may remain.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No heating, melting, acid testing or chemical treatment. Earth examples do not establish Jabberwocky history.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Evidence-case reasoning — formative.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Use three cases plus the local evidence card.

### Day 6 — Mission 2 — What History Fits Our Evidence?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open continent rock-history clues; prepare Rock History Profile.
- **MATERIALS:** Device/projector; clues; Rock History Profile.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Choose a formation pathway or **not enough evidence yet**; identify two clues, one possible later rock-cycle change and one uncertainty.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Rock history is an inference supported by evidence, not a direct observation.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Do not force a rock class where evidence is incomplete.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** **Rock History Profile — formative.**
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** One process + two clues + one uncertainty.

### Day 8 — Mission 3 — Break, Move, Settle
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare weathering cards; choose the standard Surface Change Fair Test setup and one water amount.
- **MATERIALS:** Weathering cards; tray setup preview; rulers; data sheet.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Weathering vs erosion vs deposition; gradual/sudden change; fair-test question/prediction; variable plan.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** **BREAK → MOVE → SETTLE**.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Mission 3 models gradual surface change; Earth sudden-change examples remain Earth Reference Evidence.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Prediction + fair-test plan — checkpoint evidence in progress.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Protect the variable table and BREAK/MOVE/SETTLE distinction.

### Day 9 — Mission 3 — Surface Change Fair Test
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Set shallow trays, pre-measured sediment and 50–100 mL water amount; keep devices away from water; prepare backup dataset.
- **MATERIALS:** Reusable trays; small sand/soil/gravel amount; marked cup; ruler; books/blocks; towels.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Lower-slope vs higher-slope comparison; measure transport distance; record deposition location and qualitative observation.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Fair tests change one important variable while keeping other conditions reasonably similar.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No erosion contest. One water manager per team.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Quantitative + qualitative Surface Change data — checkpoint.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Use one teacher/class demonstration plus the supplied dataset.

### Day 10 — Mission 3 — What Does the Pattern Show?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Pool selected class data; prepare anomaly case and interpolation/extrapolation task.
- **MATERIALS:** Dataset; prepared table/graph if needed; model-data task; continent clues.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Explain pattern; keep one anomalous result; interpolate/extrapolate model data; state model limits; read continent clues.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Patterns support conditional predictions; anomalies should be explained, not erased.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Do not convert classroom centimetres into exact long-term geological rates.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Pattern/anomaly/prediction reasoning — checkpoint.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Provide teacher-prepared graph/table and assess interpretation only.

### Day 11 — Mission 3 — What Should JCEC Monitor?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Return usable data; prepare Surface Change Forecast and checkpoint rubric.
- **MATERIALS:** Investigation evidence; continent clues; Surface Change Forecast.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** **CLAIM → DATA → PROCESS → LONG-TERM PREDICTION → LIMITATION**.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Monitoring recommendations connect model evidence to site evidence with uncertainty.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Assess fair testing, data, process understanding and cautious prediction—not dramatic erosion.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** **Surface Change Forecast — stronger checkpoint.**
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Accept concise bullets; cut presentation/polish.

### Day 13 — Mission 4 — When Earth’s Layers Bend and Break
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Cache Parks Canada Rockies/Cordillera evidence + USGS Dynamic Planet map; prepare simple layer/fold/fault visuals.
- **MATERIALS:** Earth Reference Evidence cards; diagrams; projector/paper.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Strata, folding, faulting, Rockies/Cordillera and qualitative global crustal patterns.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Patterns in folds/faults/mountains/earthquakes/volcanoes provide evidence for crustal movement and plate tectonics.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** A fracture is not automatically a fault; keep plate tectonics qualitative.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Short pattern explanation — formative synthesis.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** One Rockies card + one global-pattern map.

### Day 14 — Mission 4 — Fossils and Geological Time
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Cache Royal Tyrrell badlands and fossil-context sources; prepare broad geological-time strip.
- **MATERIALS:** Fossil/context cards; Royal Tyrrell evidence; time strip.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Body/trace/mold-cast evidence; context and stewardship; geological time; accumulated evidence.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Fossils can reconstruct past life/environments, but context and uncertainty matter.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No dinosaur-trivia detour; no era-boundary memorization or radiometric calculations.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Fossil-context/uncertainty response — formative synthesis.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Keep three fossil-evidence categories + context/stewardship + broad time strip.

### Day 15 — Mission 4 — Reconstruct the Deep Record
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Print/project Earth Training Cross-Section and relative-sequence rules.
- **MATERIALS:** Cross-section; event/evidence cards; pencils; optional scissors.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Reconstruct **FIRST → NEXT → LATER → MOST RECENT**; use several clues; interpret one ambiguous clue.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Relative sequence uses relationships among layers, deformation and erosion; several agreeing clues strengthen explanations.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No absolute ages.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Training cross-section sequence — formative synthesis.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Teacher preorders two events; teams reason through the rest.

### Day 16 — Mission 4 — What Happened at Our Site?
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open assigned continent Deep Record Survey File; prepare Deep Record Timeline.
- **MATERIALS:** Survey File; Deep Record Timeline.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Choose best-supported past change; cite two agreeing clues; state absent/uncertain fossil/deformation evidence honestly.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Missing fossil/fault evidence is a valid result.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Do not invent ages, active faults, fossil beds or catastrophes.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** **Deep Record Timeline — formative synthesis.**
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Relative sequence + two evidence items + one uncertainty.

### Day 18 — Mission 5 — Gather the Strongest Evidence
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open Geological Evidence Locker and known/inferred/uncertain/protect-monitor framework.
- **MATERIALS:** Evidence Locker; Site Review; pencils/device.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Review M1–M4 reminders; replace with stronger real class evidence if useful; choose three strongest evidence items from at least three missions.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Synthesis is evidence selection, not archive copying.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Locker = memory support, not answer key.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Three selected evidence items — synthesis in progress.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Preselect the four reminders; teams choose three and justify them.

### Day 19 — Mission 5 — Review the Geological Site
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Open continent concern; prepare KNOWN / INFERRED / UNCERTAIN / PROTECT-MONITOR matrix.
- **MATERIALS:** Locker; Site Review; concern card.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Sort evidence into four categories; respond to final concern; identify one useful safeguard.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Evidence-based decisions distinguish direct evidence from inference and uncertainty.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** No invented mines, deposits, faults, fossil beds, ages or hazard probabilities.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** JCEC Geological Site Review — synthesis evidence.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** One strong entry per category + the safeguard.

### Day 20 — Mission 5 — Decide the Geological Handoff
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare four handoff-status cards and small STS application box.
- **MATERIALS:** Site Review; status cards; pencils.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** **GEOLOGICAL CLAIM → EVIDENCE → SITE CONSEQUENCE → UNCERTAINTY → HANDOFF RULE**; choose tentative status; brief disturbance/resource application.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Geological handoff ≠ final human-settlement decision.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Resource/reclamation is a bounded Mission 2190 application, not a separate Planet Earth outcome.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** Tentative handoff + safeguard — major synthesis.
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Cut extended STS discussion before the handoff reasoning.

### Day 21 — Mission 5 — Prepare the Final Evidence
- **BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare one-page JCEC Geological Evidence Packet and concise scoring guide.
- **MATERIALS:** Locker; Site Review; Evidence Packet; pencils/device.
- **STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Complete packet; 3–5 sentence Council message; reflection; stop before Council.
- **KEY SCIENCE:** Scientific handoffs communicate evidence, uncertainty, stewardship and next questions clearly.
- **TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Major synthesis = geological understanding + evidence use + uncertainty + responsible handoff. Do not grade visual polish.
- **COLLECT / ASSESS:** **JCEC Geological Evidence Packet — major synthesis.**
- **IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Use Flex Day 22. Cut gallery work before core reasoning.

## Seven purposeful flex periods

- **Day 7 — Sample/classification + absence recovery:** finish M1/M2 sample reasoning; use photo/property cards for missed stations; reteach rock vs mineral / formation-process distinctions.
- **Day 12 — Mission 3 checkpoint recovery:** fair-test spillover, contradictory-data troubleshooting, anomaly explanation, prediction limitation, Surface Change Forecast completion.
- **Day 17 — Planet Earth review / optional individual science check:** rock cycle, surface processes, crustal patterns, fossils, time, uncertainty and evidence-source separation.
- **Day 22 — Geological Evidence Packet completion:** finish M5 synthesis and teacher conferences.
- **Day 23 — Absence recovery / short individual assessment:** recover missed reasoning using shared/supplied evidence.
- **Day 24 — Optional Geological Evidence gallery/review:** compare different continent handoffs and evidence choices.
- **Day 25 — Final buffer / feedback / transition:** normal interruptions, reteach, make-up and transition planning. **Do not begin the final Council unless separately approved.**

## Phase 5 Mission Materials Master List

### Geologist’s Evidence Stations
- **Preferred:** 4–6 safe existing school samples; trays; rulers; limited shared hand lenses; fingernail/copper/teacher-approved steel relative-hardness references; classification key.
- **Shared-equipment plan:** one sample set can rotate; 2–4 lenses total is workable.
- **No-purchase fallback:** photo/property-data cards + classification key.
- **Absence fallback:** same cards and reasoning sequence.
- **Cleanup/safety:** no striking/breaking samples; no loose glass plates; wash hands after dusty samples.

### Rock Cycle Evidence Lab
- **Preferred:** existing samples if useful; sample photos; rock-class cards; process cards; pencils.
- **Shared-equipment plan:** projected evidence or one card set per 2–3 teams.
- **No-purchase fallback:** full projected/printed evidence cases.
- **Absence fallback:** same cases + Rock History Profile.
- **Cleanup/safety:** no heating, melting, acid testing or chemical treatment.

### Local Alberta evidence
- **Preferred:** teacher-curated Calgary-area Alberta Geological Survey evidence card.
- **Shared:** whole-class projection.
- **No-purchase fallback:** cached/printed summary.
- **Absence:** include with missed-work packet.
- **Safety:** no collection trip required.

### Weathering evidence cards
- **Preferred:** cracked rock, freeze–thaw, root-in-crack and chemical-surface examples.
- **Shared:** projector is enough.
- **No-purchase fallback:** cards are already the default.
- **Absence:** review cards and answer the same evidence question.
- **Safety:** no cracking rocks, freeze experiments or chemical reactions required.

### Surface Change Fair Test
- **Preferred:** shallow reused trays; small sand/soil/gravel amount; standard 50–100 mL water amount; books/blocks; rulers; towels.
- **Shared:** 4–6 trays can rotate; one class demonstration tray also works.
- **No-purchase fallback:** before/after photos + supplied quantitative dataset + deposition diagram + anomaly evidence + interpolation/extrapolation task.
- **Absence:** class/supplied data with the same reasoning.
- **Cleanup/safety:** devices away from water; one water manager; wipe spills; wash hands.

### Earth Reference Evidence files
- **Preferred:** cached Parks Canada Rockies/Cordillera card; USGS Dynamic Planet map; Royal Tyrrell badlands/fossil-context cards.
- **Shared:** project once or print one set per table.
- **No-purchase fallback:** cached screenshots/text summaries.
- **Absence:** same cards + guiding questions.
- **Safety/canon:** never transfer Earth faults, fossils, earthquakes, volcanoes or deposits into Jabberwocky canon.

### Deep Record Evidence Puzzle
- **Preferred:** Earth Training Cross-Section + layer/event/fossil cards + pencils.
- **Shared:** laminated group sets or projector.
- **No-purchase fallback:** the paper/projected version already is the fallback.
- **Absence:** same cross-section and rules.
- **Safety:** no real fossils, rock saws or specialty kits.

### Geological Evidence Locker / Site Review / Evidence Packet
- **Preferred:** projected/printed Locker; four-question Site Review; handoff cards; one-page Evidence Packet.
- **Shared:** projector + one team packet.
- **No-purchase fallback:** copy headings/fields onto notebook paper.
- **Absence:** use the same Locker reminders without reopening old missions.
- **Safety:** no field collection or final physical model.

## Contingency playbook

- **No rock/mineral samples:** use built-in photo/property cards.
- **Limited hand lenses:** rotate a few lenses or use projected zoom.
- **Students over-identify from one clue:** require two pieces of evidence or allow **UNCERTAIN / NOT ENOUGH EVIDENCE YET**.
- **Hardness testing becomes confusing:** stop testing and use teacher-provided relative-hardness results.
- **Mission 2 rock classifications are ambiguous:** keep “consistent with” language and uncertainty.
- **Local Alberta evidence unavailable:** use cached AGS summary; do not invent Jabberwocky geology.
- **Sediment/tray materials unavailable:** use the complete supplied dataset/photos.
- **Surface Change Fair Test runs long:** one trial/condition + pooled/shared data.
- **Water spills/device concerns:** move devices away; print data sheets; switch to fallback data if needed.
- **Contradictory erosion data:** keep the discrepancy and explain possible uncontrolled variables.
- **Graph/data interpretation takes too long:** teacher supplies graph/table; students interpret.
- **Student absent during fair test:** use class/supplied evidence and same reasoning scaffold.
- **Students erase anomalies:** require the anomaly and one plausible explanation.
- **Students over-extrapolate:** add “if model conditions stayed similar” and ask what could change in reality.
- **Mission 4 vocabulary overwhelms:** reduce visible focus to layers → bend/break → fossils → time → uncertainty.
- **Fracture vs fault confusion:** fracture = break; fault requires evidence of movement.
- **Earth-reference volcanoes/earthquakes get transferred to Jabberwocky:** point to the evidence-source labels and stop the claim.
- **Fossils become dinosaur trivia:** require context, layer and past-environment reasoning.
- **Relative sequencing is hard:** use the common Earth Training Cross-Section and event cards first.
- **Printing/projector failure:** draw the simplified cross-section/table on the board and use notebook paper.
- **Evidence Locker becomes an answer key:** students must choose, label observation/inference and may replace reminders with stronger real evidence.
- **Geological handoff becomes a settlement vote:** display **THIS IS NOT THE FINAL HUMAN-SETTLEMENT DECISION**.
- **A class is lost:** cut extra examples/presentations/polish before core reasoning and Team Records.

| Mission | Role | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — Read the Ground | Formative | Ground Evidence Card |
| M2 — Follow the Rock Story | Formative | Rock History Profile |
| M3 — Track the Changing Surface | **Stronger checkpoint** | Surface Change Forecast |

### Recommended grading balance
If a numerical split is needed:

- **30% Mission 3 checkpoint**
- **55% Mission 5 synthesis**
- **15% optional individual Planet Earth science check**

Without the individual check: approximately **35% Mission 3 + 65% Mission 5**.

Do not heavily grade all five Team Records.

### Optional individual Planet Earth science check
Keep it short and individual. Sample targets:
- observation/property/classification and rock vs mineral,
- igneous/sedimentary/metamorphic formation processes,
- weathering vs erosion vs deposition,
- controlled variables and quantitative/qualitative evidence,
- anomalies and cautious extrapolation,
- strata/folding/faulting/crustal movement,
- fossils/context/geological time,
- uncertainty/accumulated evidence,
- Earth Reference Evidence vs Jabberwocky Survey Evidence.

## Evidence-source rule

**EARTH REFERENCE EVIDENCE = real-Earth science examples.**

**JABBERWOCKY SURVEY EVIDENCE = fictional JCEC site observations.**

Never transfer an Earth earthquake, volcano, fault, fossil bed, exact age, resource deposit or hazard into Jabberwocky canon unless the JCEC survey evidence explicitly supports it.

### Approved authoritative reference sources
- Alberta Grade 7 Science Program: https://education.alberta.ca/media/159716/sci7to9.pdf
- Alberta Geological Survey — Calgary-area sand and gravel: https://ags.aer.ca/publications/all-publications/ofr-1981-08
- Parks Canada — Canadian Rocky Mountain Parks: https://parks.canada.ca/culture/spm-whs/sites-canada/sec02h
- USGS — Dynamic Planet: https://www.usgs.gov/maps/dynamic-planet-world-map-volcanoes-earthquakes-impact-craters-and-plate-tectonics

- Royal Tyrrell Museum — Found a Fossil?: https://www.tyrrellmuseum.com/research/found_a_fossil

### Fossil stewardship safeguard
Keep fossil context/location scientifically important. Do not turn Mission 4 into fossil collecting. Preserve the Royal Tyrrell guidance that buried fossils should be left in place and reported according to provincial/Museum guidance.
