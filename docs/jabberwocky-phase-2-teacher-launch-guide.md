# Jabberwocky: Mission 2190 — Phase 2 Teacher Launch Guide

## THE LIVING RESOURCE — Plants for Food & Fibre

This guide matches the completed student-facing Phase 2 website.

**Student path:**

1. **Mission 1 — Find the Living Resource — 3 classes**
2. **Mission 2 — Keep It Growing — 4 classes**
3. **Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone — 4 classes**
4. **Mission 4 — Choose the Next Generation — 3 classes**
5. **Mission 5 — Use It Without Losing It — 4 classes**

**Total:** 18 core classes + 7 purposeful flex / review / assessment / interruption periods in a 25-period unit.

Student routine remains:

**1 · YOUR MISSION → 2 · LEARN THE SCIENCE → 3 · INVESTIGATE → 4 · MAKE A DECISION → 5 · RECORD IT**

Collaboration remains:

**TEAM WORK → TEAM RECORD → MY REFLECTION**

The teacher manages team/continent assignments, backend metadata, submission logistics, and assessment tracking. Students should not have to manage those systems.

---

# Before Day 1

## Set the system once

- Create new Phase 2 teams and assign each team a Phase 2 continent, normally different from its Phase 1 posting.
- Keep the Phase 2 continent state separate from Phase 1. The student site uses `jabberwocky-phase2-posting`.
- Decide whether Team Records will be paper, digital, or both.
- Print or stage only the materials needed for the upcoming mission rather than distributing a full-unit packet.
- Prepare one small **teacher backup Growth Trial** 2–3 days before Mission 2 if practical.
- Choose **one changed Growth Trial condition for the whole class**. Prefer a no-purchase condition such as reduced water or reduced light unless there is a reason to use something else.
- Decide where Growth Trial containers will stay for approximately 7–10 days.
- Use **shared stations, reused containers, printables and ordinary classroom supplies first**. Avoid buying a full duplicate lab kit for every team when students can rotate or share.

## Phase 2 teacher launch rule

Do not teach the whole unit up front. Each mission should feel like the next problem JCEC has discovered.

# 18 core classes

## Mission 1 — Find the Living Resource — 3 classes

### Day 1 — New Team, New Resource Plant

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Assign Phase 2 teams/continents. Open the Phase 2 Hub. Set up 3–5 shared flowering/seed-plant stations, or one demonstration station if that is all that is available. Print Mission 1 Plant Structure Investigation sheets.

**MATERIALS**  
Devices; a few shared real flowering/seed plants; pencils; Plant Structure Investigation sheet. Hand lenses are optional; trays are only needed if specimens are messy.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Phase 2 launch → new-team / new-continent handoff → Previous Team Briefing → Mission 1 briefing → learn roots, stems, leaves, reproductive structures → begin real-plant investigation.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Roots absorb/anchor; stems support/transport; leaves capture light and support photosynthesis/gas exchange; flowers/reproductive structures support reproduction.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Use **structure → job → survival**. Rotate teams through shared plant stations rather than purchasing one specimen per team.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Plant Structure Investigation observations — formative only.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Finish the structure-to-job table next class. Cut extended whole-class sharing before cutting observation time.

### Day 2 — From Earth Plant to Jabberwocky Plant

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Return/retain field sheets. Confirm each team device has the correct Phase 2 continent posting. Open Mission 1 continent resource-plant clues.

**MATERIALS**  
Devices; Mission 1 field sheet; Living Resource Profile; pencils.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Finish real-plant reasoning if needed → investigate four structure clues for the assigned Jabberwocky plant → connect structure, function, adaptation, and possible human use.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Plant structures vary; structural features can help plants survive particular environments; useful plants remain part of ecosystems.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
The fictional plant must still obey the science students just observed. Avoid turning the task into creative creature/plant design.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Check that at least one structure claim is supported by a clue — formative.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Do not open optional future-production details. Protect the four clues and structure/function reasoning.

### Day 3 — Living Resource Decision

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Make Living Resource Profile available. Confirm inherited Phase 1 warning is visible on the site.

**MATERIALS**  
Device; Living Resource Profile; Mission 1 evidence.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Answer one team question: which plant feature makes the resource most promising or difficult for humans to use? Complete one profile + individual reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Adaptations help a plant survive but can also shape how humans might use or grow it.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
One strong feature with a clear explanation is enough. Assess science reasoning, not artistic polish.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
**Living Resource Profile — formative.** Reflection may be checked for understanding but need not be graded.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Cut polish / sharing. Keep the team decision, profile, and reflection.

## Mission 2 — Keep It Growing — 4 classes

### Day 4 — What Does a Plant Need? Launch the Growth Trial

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Choose the single class-wide changed condition. Prefer reduced water or reduced light to keep the setup simple and inexpensive. Stage two small reused containers per team, fast-growing seeds, growing medium, labels and water. If possible, have one teacher backup tray already started.

**MATERIALS**  
2 small reused containers/team; fast-growing seeds; potting/growing medium; labels/markers; water; measuring cup/spoon; tray or spill mat; Growth Trial data sheet. Extra treatment materials are only needed if you choose a treatment that requires them.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Mission launch → basic plant needs → set up CONTROL and CHANGED conditions → record starting state.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Plant growth depends on environmental conditions. A fair comparison changes one condition while keeping other important conditions similar.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Use the same changed condition across the class so pooled evidence is interpretable. Clean yogurt cups or similar reused containers are fine. The goal is evidence, not specialized equipment.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Growth Trial setup/data sheet — formative evidence only, not a separate assignment.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Teacher can finish watering/placement logistics after class. Do not skip labels or fair-test discussion.

### Day 5 — Get and Move Water and Materials

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Check Growth Trial containers. Prepare a very short coloured-water transport or transpiration demonstration only if it adds value and materials are already available.

**MATERIALS**  
Devices; Growth Trial sheet; ruler; optional clear cup, coloured water, celery/carnation or simple transpiration demo materials.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Learn **GET MATERIALS**: diffusion + osmosis → **MOVE WATER & MATERIALS**: conduction/transport + transpiration → 3–5 minute Growth Trial observation.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Water and dissolved materials enter/move through plants; water can leave leaves as vapour.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Treat these as one connected pathway, not four isolated vocabulary words. Detailed xylem/phloem anatomy is optional enrichment.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Quick observation + verbal/exit explanation — formative.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Cut the optional demonstration before cutting the connected process explanation or growth check.

### Day 6 — Make Food and Make New Plants

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Check plants; prepare a simple life-cycle visual.

**MATERIALS**  
Devices; Growth Trial sheet; ruler; simple seed/seedling/flower images or actual examples if available.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Learn photosynthesis + gas exchange → seed → germination → seedling → mature plant → flowers/seeds → simple propagation → 3–5 minute Growth Trial observation.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Photosynthesis uses light, water, and carbon dioxide to make food; oxygen is released. Plants reproduce and new plants can begin from seeds or some vegetative methods.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Keep photosynthesis conceptual. Do not add detailed cellular chemistry or Grade 9 genetics.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Quick process explanation — formative.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Keep photosynthesis, gas exchange, germination and life-cycle sequence. Shorten propagation examples.

### Day 7 — What Will Work on Jabberwocky?

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Confirm each continent's four growing clues are available. Make Plant Growth Requirements Card available.

**MATERIALS**  
Device; Growth Trial sheet; Plant Growth Requirements Card.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Compare available Growth Trial evidence → read only assigned continent clues → decide which growing condition humans must control most carefully → complete Team Record + reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Plant processes interact with environmental conditions; growth needs vary by context.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Use: **When ___ changed, ___ changed. This suggests the plant needs ___. On our continent, this matters because ___.**

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
**Plant Growth Requirements Card — formative.** Growth Trial remains active.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Complete the card next class/flex. Do not end or discard the Growth Trial simply because Mission 2's card is complete.

## Mission 3 — Build the Growing Zone — 4 classes

### Day 9 — What Makes Good Soil?

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Set up shared stations with small class quantities of sand-rich, clay-rich and organic-rich media. Reuse identical cups/filters or drainage containers at the stations.

**MATERIALS**  
Three shared media; reusable drainage cups/containers; trays; water; measuring cups/cylinders; spoons; one or a few timers; paper towels; soil/growing-medium investigation sheet.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Mission launch → soil particle size → spaces between particles → drainage, retention, compaction → begin growing-medium investigation.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Particle size and pore spaces affect how water moves and how roots experience the growing medium.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Connect the words causally: **particles → spaces → water movement → root conditions**. Teams rotate through stations rather than receiving three complete material sets.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Investigation observations — formative.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Finish drainage measurements next class. Cut extra media comparisons first.

### Day 10 — Investigate the Growing Medium

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Reset shared media stations if necessary. Prepare short examples/cards for fertilizer, irrigation, protected growing, raised/contained beds, and soilless/hydroponic growing.

**MATERIALS**  
Shared soil investigation stations; technology cards/images; devices.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Complete growing-medium tests → compare evidence → learn how fertilizer, irrigation, and modified growing environments can solve problems and create new ones.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Plants need water and dissolved nutrients/materials. Human technologies can change growing conditions; too much fertilizer/water or poorly matched systems can cause impacts.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Technologies are tools for specific problems, not automatically better methods.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Completed soil/growing-medium evidence — formative.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Keep one clear example each of water, nutrient, and environment modification. Move optional technology detail to Learn More.

### Day 11 — Build the Growing Zone

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Take final/near-final Growth Trial reading if appropriate. Set out one shared prototype-material station. Provide one reused cup/container per team if physically building, or let teams use a labelled design instead. Confirm continent growing challenge cards.

**MATERIALS**  
Growth Trial sheets; rulers; one reused cup/container per team if building; shared media; gravel; wick/string; cover material; water; safe drainage setup; continent evidence. A labelled design is an equally valid low-material option.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Compare control vs changed-condition evidence → read 3–4 continent growing clues → build/test one small growing-system model OR create a labelled testable design that solves one problem.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Evidence about plant needs and media behaviour can guide a practical growing-system choice; solutions can also create consequences.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
One problem, one testable idea, one consequence. Do not create a miniature engineering kit for every team; the reasoning is the checkpoint.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Prototype/design evidence and Growth Trial comparison — checkpoint evidence in progress.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Use a labelled design instead of a physical build. Protect the evidence-to-decision connection.

### Day 12 — Recommend a Growing System

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Make Growing System Recommendation available. Return soil/Growth Trial evidence.

**MATERIALS**  
Device; growing-medium evidence; Growth Trial evidence; prototype/design; Growing System Recommendation.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Refine recommendation → identify one possible environmental consequence → add one safeguard → complete Team Record + reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
A practical growing system should fit plant needs and environmental conditions while reducing foreseeable impacts.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Reasoning frame: **Our growing system should ___ because our evidence shows ___. One possible problem is ___, so we would ___.**

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
**Growing System Recommendation — stronger Phase 2 reasoning checkpoint.** Assess science understanding, evidence, reasoning, and practical decision.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Accept a clear diagram + concise reasoning. Cut presentation/polish before cutting consequence + safeguard.

## Mission 4 — Choose the Next Generation — 3 classes

### Day 14 — Plants Are Not All the Same

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Print/cut Selective Breeding Simulation starter plant cards and generation tracker.

**MATERIALS**  
Starter population cards; trait labels; generation tracker; devices.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Mission launch → reproduction/pollination → plant variety → selective breeding → choose one target trait → begin simulation.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Plants of the same kind vary. Humans can repeatedly choose plants with useful characteristics to reproduce so those characteristics become more common in cultivated varieties.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Variation exists **before** selection. Do not introduce Punnett squares, alleles, genotype/phenotype, or dominant/recessive inheritance.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Simulation tracker — formative/light.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Model one generation together and continue next class.

### Day 15 — Choose the Next Generation

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Prepare continent-appropriate surprise event cards and short pest-management examples.

**MATERIALS**  
Simulation cards/tracker; surprise cards; simple biological / physical-cultural / chemical control cards.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Run 2–3 simplified generations → observe trait change → reveal pest/environment surprise → discuss monoculture/low-variety risk → consider simple control strategies.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Selective breeding can increase useful traits; very uniform populations may share vulnerabilities. Pest-management methods can have benefits and unintended effects.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Selective breeding is useful, not automatically harmful. **Variety and safeguards still matter.** Real offspring vary and real breeding takes many generations.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Simulation reasoning — formative/light.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Stop after two generations. Reveal one surprise. Do not add a second pest scenario.

### Day 16 — What Should JCEC Select?

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Open three continent evidence cards. Make Plant Variety Decision Card available.

**MATERIALS**  
Device; simulation result; continent evidence; Plant Variety Decision Card.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Use one simulation result + continent evidence → answer one team question about the trait JCEC should favour and the risk it must watch → complete Team Record + reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Useful traits involve trade-offs; the best choice depends on environmental conditions and risks.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Use: **We would select ___ because ___. But we would need to watch for ___.**

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
**Plant Variety Decision Card — formative/light.**

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Cut whole-class sharing. Keep trait + evidence + risk + safeguard.

## Mission 5 — Use It Without Losing It — 4 classes

### Day 18 — How Big Can This Get?

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Print continent Production Footprint maps/zone mats and paper planning tiles. Confirm each team sees its continent/resource plant and inherited warning.

**MATERIALS**  
Printed continent footprint map; paper growing/harvest tiles; protected habitat tiles; water-access tiles; road/infrastructure tiles; processing/storage tiles; buffer/protection tiles; devices.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Mission launch → land use, production inputs, variety/resilience, sustainability → receive 6-zone map, one production goal, and 3 constraints → begin resource-use plan.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Land conversion, irrigation, fertilizer, pest management, infrastructure, and harvesting can increase production and also affect habitat, soil, water, and resilience.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Students are answering **Can we keep producing it without damaging the system?**, not only **Can we produce it?** This is intentionally a paper planning model, not a construction project.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Initial footprint plan — formative evidence for final synthesis.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Use fewer tiles. Do not add more map zones or constraints.

### Day 19 — The Consequence

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Prepare one continent-appropriate consequence card per team.

**MATERIALS**  
Printed footprint maps/tiles; consequence cards; pencils.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Complete initial plan → reveal consequence → identify what the consequence exposes → revise exactly one part of the plan.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Resource systems can create unintended effects; sustainable planning includes monitoring, buffers, limits, and revision.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
A consequence is not a punishment for a wrong answer. It tests whether the plan can adapt.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
One justified revision — formative evidence.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Teacher can reveal the consequence to the whole class and require one written revision rather than a second physical layout.

### Day 20 — Build the Recommendation

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Open Mission 5 Evidence Locker. Ensure earlier Team Records are available only if needed for clarification. Print JCEC Living Resource Plans.

**MATERIALS**  
Device; Evidence Locker; footprint result; JCEC Living Resource Plan.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Review four short evidence reminders → choose 3 strongest pieces, preferably from different missions → select final strategy → begin Living Resource Plan.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Sustainable plant-resource decisions require evidence about the plant, its needs, growing system, variation/risk, and environmental consequences.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Students should not reread four missions. The Evidence Locker is the default. Three strong evidence items beat a long list.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
Living Resource Plan draft — major synthesis in progress.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Protect evidence selection + final strategy. Move polishing to Day 21/22.

### Day 21 — JCEC Living Resource Decision

**BEFORE CLASS**  
Prepare final collection/submission method and concise teacher rubric.

**MATERIALS**  
JCEC Living Resource Plan; Evidence Locker; footprint revision; pencils/devices.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO**  
Complete one-page plan → final 3–5 sentence explanation → individual reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE**  
Human needs, technologies, plant biology, environmental conditions, and sustainability are connected.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS**  
Assess **science understanding → evidence → reasoning → sustainable practical decision**. Visual polish should carry little or no weight.

**COLLECT / ASSESS**  
**JCEC Living Resource Plan — major Phase 2 synthesis.** Individual reflection is supporting evidence.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT**  
Use Day 22 for essential completion. Cut presentations/gallery walk first.

# Growth Trial management timeline

## Recommended setup

Run one **class-wide changed condition** so teams can compare/pool evidence. Students compare **CONTROL** with **CHANGED** and record only:

- germination
- plant height
- number of leaves
- visible plant health

Quick observations should take **3–5 minutes** and remain evidence rather than separate submissions.

Use **reused small containers** wherever practical. Reduced water or reduced light are recommended default changed conditions because they require no additional specialized material.

## Timeline

**2–3 days before Day 4 (teacher only, optional):** Start one backup tray using the same seed type.  
**Day 4:** Set up team CONTROL + CHANGED containers.  
**Day 5:** Check 1.  
**Day 6:** Check 2.  
**Day 7:** Check 3 / Mission 2 reasoning. Keep plants growing.  
**Day 8 flex:** Extra observation if useful.  
**Days 9–10:** Quick observations while Mission 3 begins.  
**Day 11:** Final or near-final measurement; compare conditions.  
**Day 12:** Use Growth Trial evidence in the Growing System Recommendation.  
**Day 13 flex:** Use only if biological timing needs another final check or graph.

## Growth Trial contingencies

**Failed germination**  
Use teacher backup plants, pooled class data, or a teacher-provided sample dataset. Students should still interpret evidence and discuss limits.

**Delayed germination**  
Use Day 8 and/or Day 13 flex. Do not delay the entire unit if enough evidence exists to reason from.

**Little/no difference between conditions**  
Treat this as valid evidence. Ask: Was the treatment strong enough? Was there enough time? What can the data support? Do not tell students the trial “failed” because the expected result did not appear.

**Weekend / holiday interruption**  
Teacher manages watering/light placement as needed. Students do not need daily data. The required evidence is a few well-spaced observations.

# Simplified Phase 2 assessment map

## Formative — no major grade

**Mission 1 — Living Resource Profile**  
Use to diagnose structure/function/adaptation understanding.

**Mission 2 — Plant Growth Requirements Card**  
Use to diagnose plant-process and evidence interpretation.

**Mission 4 — Plant Variety Decision Card**  
Use to diagnose selective-breeding / trade-off reasoning.

These can receive feedback, completion checks, or brief rubric notes. Avoid turning them into three extra projects.

## Stronger reasoning checkpoint

**Mission 3 — Growing System Recommendation**

Recommended teacher criteria:

1. **Science understanding** — does the proposed system fit plant/soil/environment science?
2. **Evidence** — does the team use soil and/or Growth Trial evidence accurately?
3. **Reasoning** — is the link between evidence and recommendation clear?
4. **Practical decision** — does the team identify a consequence and a useful safeguard?

## Major synthesis

**Mission 5 — JCEC Living Resource Plan**

Recommended teacher criteria:

1. **Science understanding**
2. **Use of evidence from across Phase 2**
3. **Reasoning**
4. **Sustainable practical decision**

Visual polish should have little or no weight.

## Recommended grading balance

When a formal Phase 2 grade is needed:

- **30% — Mission 3 Growing System Recommendation**
- **55% — Mission 5 JCEC Living Resource Plan**
- **15% — short individual science check (optional but recommended if individual evidence is needed)**

Missions 1, 2, and 4 remain formative. If the individual science check is not needed, redistribute locally rather than creating another large assignment.

# Alberta Grade 7 Plants for Food & Fibre alignment

This teacher system is aligned to the existing Alberta Grade 7 Science Program of Studies, **Unit B — Plants for Food & Fibre**. Official source: `https://education.alberta.ca/media/159716/sci7to9.pdf`.

The four Unit B general outcome areas are represented as follows:

| Alberta Unit B outcome area | Where Phase 2 addresses it | Student evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Investigate plant uses; identify links among needs, technologies, products and impacts** | Missions 1, 3, 5 | Living Resource Profile; Growing System Recommendation; Living Resource Plan |
| **2. Investigate life processes and structures of plants; interpret characteristics and needs** | Missions 1–2 | real-plant structure investigation; Growth Trial; Plant Growth Requirements Card |
| **3. Analyze plant environments; identify impacts of specific factors and controls** | Missions 2–5 | Growth Trial; soil/growing-medium evidence; growing-system decision; selective-breeding/pest simulation; Production Footprint Challenge |
| **4. Interpret relationships among human needs, technologies, environments and use of living things for food/fibre** | Missions 3–5 | Growing System Recommendation; Plant Variety Decision; JCEC Living Resource Plan |

### Required science deliberately preserved

- plant structures/functions: roots, stems, leaves, reproductive structures
- structural variation/adaptation
- plant needs and environmental tolerances
- diffusion, osmosis, conduction/transport, transpiration
- photosynthesis and gas exchange at a general Grade 7 level
- seed-plant life cycle, germination, reproduction, propagation
- soil characteristics: particle size, drainage, retention, compaction
- practices/technologies that change growing environments
- nutrients/fertilizer at an introductory level
- irrigation and protected/contained/soilless growing as practical technologies
- plant varieties and selective breeding
- monoculture / low-variety risk
- pest-management approaches and possible unintended effects
- food/fibre/resource use, land use, environmental impacts and sustainability

### Kept optional / enrichment

- detailed xylem/phloem anatomy
- cellular photosynthesis chemistry
- advanced soil chemistry
- genetics terminology/Punnett squares
- detailed pesticide chemistry
- agricultural economics

Examples in the Alberta Program of Studies are illustrative rather than additional required outcomes. Hydroponics, for example, is treated here as one possible growing-environment technology, not a separate mandatory unit.

# Contingency playbook

## Failed germination

Use teacher backup plants, pooled class results, or a teacher-provided dataset. Preserve the evidence-analysis question. Do not spend several classes restarting the whole unit.

## Missing lab materials

**Mission 1:** use one demonstration plant or a small shared station rotation.  
**Mission 2:** use reused containers and a no-purchase water/light treatment; if needed, use teacher-demo containers + pooled class trial.  
**Mission 3:** test only two media or use prepared drainage/retention data; the growing-system checkpoint can use a labelled design instead of a physical prototype.  
**Mission 4:** display plant cards digitally or use projected cards.  
**Mission 5:** use printed or hand-drawn paper zones/tiles.

## A class is lost

Use the nearest flex day first. If no flex remains, combine the lightest adjacent tasks:

- M1 Days 2–3: shorten clue discussion; complete decision/profile in same period.
- M2 Days 5–6: teach the three plant-process jobs in one structured lesson; keep Growth Trial observation short.
- M3 Days 9–10: teacher-prestage soil tests; reduce to the most useful measurements.
- M4 Days 14–15: run only two simplified generations before the surprise.
- M5 Days 18–19: use a prebuilt first footprint and ask teams to evaluate/revise it.

Protect the final evidence-based team decision in every mission.

## Student absent during a lab

Do not require a solo repeat unless practical. Give the student team/class data, a photo of the setup, and one focused interpretation question. The assessment target is science reasoning, not attendance at a particular apparatus.

## Growth Trial shows little difference

Use the actual result. Ask what the evidence does and does not support. Compare pooled class data if helpful. Avoid retrofitting the conclusion students were “supposed” to get.

## Selective-breeding simulation runs long

Stop after two generations. Reveal one surprise event. The key learning is repeated selection + reduced variety risk, not the number of rounds.

## Production Footprint Challenge takes too long

Freeze the current plan, reveal the consequence, and require one written/diagrammed revision. Do not require teams to rebuild the entire map.

## Students are overwhelmed

Return to the mission question and one scaffold:

- M1: **structure → job → survival**
- M2: **condition changed → plant response → plant need**
- M3: **system → evidence → consequence → safeguard**
- M4: **select trait → benefit → risk**

# Teacher launch-readiness audit

## Do I know what to prepare tomorrow?

**Yes.** Every core day identifies setup and materials, with shared/printable defaults where the science allows them.

## Do I know what students should finish each day?

**Yes.** The core-day plan names the student task and collection point. Only Missions 3 and 5 carry significant formal assessment weight.

## Do I know what I actually need to assess?

**Yes.** Missions 1, 2 and 4 are formative; Mission 3 is the reasoning checkpoint; Mission 5 is the major synthesis; the short individual science check is optional/targeted.

## Can I recover if plant growth or lab timing changes?

**Yes.** Days 8 and 13 are deliberately positioned around the Growth Trial and Mission 3. The contingency playbook allows backup data, pooled data, shared stations, labelled designs, fewer simulation rounds, and written revisions without sacrificing the learning target.

## Do the teacher guide and student website describe the same Phase 2 experience?

**Yes.** Both use the same five mission names, 3 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4 core-class pacing, investigations, Team Records, reasoning scaffolds, and final JCEC Living Resource Plan.

**Stop after Phase 2. Do not begin Phase 3 until the Phase 2 teacher system is approved.**
