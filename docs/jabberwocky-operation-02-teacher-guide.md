# Jabberwocky Phase 1 — Operation 02 Teacher Guide

## Environmental Survey

### Purpose
Operation 02 establishes the hands-on science routine for Mission 2190. Students first collect authentic environmental evidence in a real Earth microhabitat, then apply the same observational reasoning to fictional JCEC telemetry from their assigned continent.

The fiction is the application layer. The real field investigation is essential because students need experience observing, measuring, comparing, and recognizing uncertainty in an actual environment.

## Alberta Grade 7 Science alignment
This operation supports the Interactions and Ecosystems work by having students:
- distinguish biotic and abiotic components of environments;
- investigate environmental conditions that affect organisms and habitats;
- collect and record qualitative and quantitative observations;
- compare evidence from multiple sample sites;
- use evidence to make inferences about where organisms may live;
- recognize that environmental conclusions depend on the evidence available and may need revision;
- begin environmental monitoring practices that will be revisited later in Phase 1.

This operation intentionally does **not** reveal the named Jabberwocky native species or later ecological incidents. Operation 03 remains the first deeper investigation of organism adaptations and niches.

## Recommended timing
### Class 1 — Earth Field Certification (45–60 min)
1. Mission briefing / biotic vs abiotic refresher — 8–10 min
2. Assign team field roles — 3–5 min
3. Outdoor or approved-site survey — 25–35 min
4. Quick comparison / cleanup — 8–10 min

### Class 2 — Jabberwocky Landing-Zone Analysis (45–60 min)
1. Revisit field evidence — 5 min
2. Landing-zone telemetry + Evidence Scanner — 15–20 min
3. Team analysis and consensus — 15 min
4. One Continental Archive submission per team — 10 min
5. Individual Pathfinder reflection — 5 min

The two classes can be compressed if students already understand biotic/abiotic factors or extended if you want more repeated sampling.

## Materials per team
Minimum:
- 1 thermometer
- clipboard / hard writing surface
- pencil
- metre stick, string square, or four temporary markers for about 1 m²
- one printed field sheet

Optional:
- hand lens
- light meter or Chromebook-compatible sensor
- soil-moisture meter
- small flags or cones for sample points

If no electronic sensors are available, the operation already supports qualitative categories for light and soil moisture.

## Suggested team roles
- **Instrument Specialist** — handles measurements and reports values.
- **Recorder** — records all observations and units.
- **Organism Observer** — searches carefully for living things and signs of life.
- **Quality Checker** — checks units, repeated samples, and whether claims match the evidence.

Rotate roles during later investigations so students do not stay in the same job all year.

## Field-site options
Preferred: schoolyard or nearby school-approved outdoor space.

Useful contrasting microhabitats might include:
- open grass vs shaded grass;
- tree base vs open field;
- garden edge vs compacted path edge;
- sunny wall vs shaded wall;
- naturalized area vs mowed area.

If weather or access prevents outdoor work, use three contrasting indoor microhabitats such as near a sunny window, a planted area, and a shaded/low-traffic area. The investigation is stronger outdoors, but the reasoning structure still works indoors.

## Safety and stewardship
Students should:
- remain inside teacher-approved boundaries;
- avoid tasting, picking, or handling unknown organisms;
- minimize disturbance to organisms and habitats;
- avoid unsafe surfaces, traffic areas, standing water, or known hazards;
- wash hands after fieldwork.

The activity should model low-impact ecological observation rather than collecting organisms unnecessarily.

## Required student evidence
Each team should leave the field investigation with three sample records including:
- location description;
- temperature;
- light condition;
- moisture condition;
- ground/surface description;
- organisms or evidence of organisms;
- human impacts.

Students then identify which sampled microhabitat appears strongest and support that conclusion with at least two pieces of evidence.

## Jabberwocky application
The page automatically loads the continent stored from Operation 01. Each continent receives different but comparable environmental telemetry and six evidence observations.

Students classify evidence as biotic or abiotic, then answer a continent-specific investigation question. The important reasoning is not the classification game itself; it is the team explanation of how environmental conditions may affect habitat and organism distribution.

## Continental Archive submission
One student submits for the team through the JCEC Submission Portal.

Use:
- **Submission Type:** Continental Archive
- **Mission Phase:** 01 First Contact
- **Operation / Task:** Environmental Survey — Landing Site Profile
- **Continent:** assigned continent
- **Team ID:** phase + continent + team number, e.g. `P1-GY-01`
- **Team Members:** everyone represented by the submission

The archive entry should contain:
1. a concise main finding about the landing environment;
2. at least three pieces of evidence including biotic and abiotic evidence;
3. reasoning about habitat and limiting factors;
4. one important uncertainty / next question.

## Individual accountability
After the team archive is submitted, every student completes the Pathfinder reflection:
- Which evidence mattered most?
- Did the evidence make the continent seem more suitable, less suitable, or more uncertain than the first prediction?
- What should Operation 03 investigate next?

This reflection belongs in the Pathfinder Log by default. It can be collected through JCEC selectively if individual evidence is needed for assessment.

## Assessment opportunities
Low-stakes/formative evidence can include:
- accurate measurements and units;
- distinction between observation and inference;
- correct biotic/abiotic classification;
- quality of evidence selected for the archive;
- strength of the explanation connecting environment to habitat;
- ability to identify uncertainty rather than pretending the evidence proves more than it does.

Suggested recurring rubric dimensions:
- Science Understanding
- Evidence
- Reasoning
- Communication / Decision-Making

## Next narrative step
Operation 02 answers: **What kind of environment is this?**

Operation 03 should answer: **What kind of organisms can survive here, and how do their adaptations and niches fit the environment?**

Do not reveal later invasive-species, contamination, succession, or final settlement twists during this operation.
