# Jabberwocky Phase 1 — Operation 04 Teacher Guide

## Ecosystem Network

### Purpose
Operation 04 expands students from one organism to a connected ecosystem. Students first build a physical Earth food web with reusable cards and string, then analyze continent-specific Jabberwocky evidence to create **Food Web V1** for their Continental Archive.

The operation emphasizes that an ecosystem is a network of interdependent relationships. Students work with producers, consumers, decomposers, feeding relationships, energy flow, competition, symbiosis, and predictions about change.

The physical string model is deliberately simple. It is used to make interdependence visible and tactile before students interpret a more complex fictional ecosystem.

## Alberta Grade 7 Science alignment
This operation supports the Interactions and Ecosystems work by having students:
- identify producers, consumers, and decomposers;
- construct and interpret food chains and food webs;
- trace energy through feeding relationships;
- distinguish energy flow from the cycling of matter;
- examine competition for limited resources;
- examine symbiotic relationships, including examples of mutualism, commensalism, and parasitism;
- identify interdependence among organisms;
- predict direct and indirect effects when one part of an ecosystem changes;
- recognize that food-web models simplify real ecosystems and that predictions contain uncertainty;
- communicate evidence-based ecological explanations.

Operation 04 intentionally does **not** become the full population-data investigation. Operation 05 will use graphs and changing population evidence in much more depth.

## Recommended timing
### Class 1 — String Web Investigation (45–60 min)
1. Mission briefing / producer-consumer-decomposer refresher — 8–10 min
2. Build the Earth string web — 15–20 min
3. Arrow-direction and decomposer discussion — 8–10 min
4. Network stress test — 10–15 min
5. Debrief model limits — 5 min

### Class 2 — Continental Food Web V1 (45–60 min)
1. Revisit the Operation 03 focal species — 5 min
2. Classify continental organism records — 10–15 min
3. Convert feeding evidence into a food web — 15–20 min
4. Symbiosis + competition analysis — 10 min
5. Begin change challenge — 5–10 min

### Optional Class 3 — Network reasoning and archive (30–60 min)
Use if you want students to:
- refine Food Web V1;
- compare different team webs;
- complete a short cross-continent symbiosis share;
- orally defend one direct and one indirect effect;
- submit the archive summary and complete individual reflection with less time pressure.

## Materials per team
Minimum:
- one reusable set of 8 Earth ecosystem cards;
- several short pieces of yarn/string;
- paper arrowheads, sticky-note arrows, or pencils to mark arrow direction;
- one Operation 04 field packet / Food Web V1 sheet;
- pencil.

Recommended prep:
- print and laminate one Earth card set per team;
- store each set with short pieces of yarn in a labelled envelope or zipper bag;
- reuse the materials each year.

## Earth String Web setup
Cards:
- Sunlight — energy source, not an organism
- Prairie Grass — producer
- Wildflower — producer
- Grasshopper — consumer
- Rabbit — consumer
- Mouse — consumer
- Hawk — consumer
- Fungi & Bacteria — decomposers

Suggested feeding connections:
- Sunlight → Prairie Grass
- Sunlight → Wildflower
- Prairie Grass → Grasshopper
- Prairie Grass → Rabbit
- Prairie Grass → Mouse
- Wildflower → Grasshopper
- Wildflower → Rabbit
- Grasshopper → Mouse
- Mouse → Hawk
- Rabbit → Hawk

For decomposers, avoid implying that energy returns to producers. Students should show that dead material and wastes from organisms can become resources for decomposers. The useful conceptual distinction is:
- **energy flows** through the ecosystem and is eventually released as heat;
- **matter cycles** and can be reused.

## Arrow-direction misconception
A very common student error is to draw arrows from predator to prey because they read an arrow as “eats.”

Use the wording:
**Food-web arrows show the direction of energy transfer: food → eater.**

Example:
Grass → Rabbit means energy stored in the grass can move to the rabbit when the rabbit eats it.

## Network Stress Test
Temporarily remove Prairie Grass from the physical model.

Ask students to identify:
1. direct effects — organisms that lose a resource immediately;
2. indirect effects — organisms that could be affected later through another population;
3. possible competition shifts — consumers relying more heavily on Wildflower;
4. uncertainty — what additional information would be needed to predict actual population changes.

Important: do not let students present the card-removal model as proof. The activity produces **predictions**, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Continental network workflow
Each continent has:
- a focal species from Operation 03;
- producer records;
- consumer records;
- a decomposer record;
- several evidence-supported feeding links;
- one competition case;
- one symbiosis case;
- one simple change challenge.

Students should:
1. classify each organism as producer, consumer, or decomposer;
2. use the feeding evidence to draw arrows from food → eater;
3. add the decomposer to the web;
4. identify the focal species and its connections;
5. record the competition relationship and shared resource;
6. classify the symbiosis case;
7. predict one direct and one indirect response to the change challenge;
8. identify one uncertainty.

## Symbiosis reference
Students should use the evidence rather than memorizing a name without reasoning.

- **Mutualism:** both species benefit.
- **Commensalism:** one benefits and the other has no clear benefit or harm.
- **Parasitism:** one benefits and the other is harmed.

Not every continent has the same symbiosis type. This is intentional. A quick cross-continent share can expose the class to multiple forms of symbiosis.

## Competition
Competition is not a feeding arrow. Students should annotate it beside the food web.

Strong explanations identify:
- the two organisms;
- the shared limited resource;
- why changing resource availability could change the strength of competition.

## Food Web V1 requirements
One official team drawing should include:
1. every organism record from the continent file;
2. P / C / D role labels;
3. feeding arrows from food → eater;
4. the focal species clearly marked;
5. competition + shared resource;
6. symbiosis partners + type;
7. a decomposer connection;
8. one direct-effect prediction;
9. one indirect-effect prediction;
10. one uncertainty.

The paper Food Web V1 becomes part of the Continental Archive. The Google Form submission is the written reasoning summary, not an upload of the drawing.

## Continental interpretation guide
These are intended ecological relationships, not a required drawing layout.

### Gyre — Ridgeback Grazer
Core network:
- Bluecrest Grass → Ridgeback Grazer
- Bluecrest Grass → Valley Hopper
- Basin Bloom → Ridge Midge
- Basin Bloom → Valley Hopper
- Valley Hopper → Crag Hunter
- Ridgeback Grazer → Crag Hunter (primarily young individuals)
- Stonecap Fungus decomposes dead material/waste.

Competition:
- Ridgeback Grazer + Valley Hopper for Bluecrest Grass.

Symbiosis:
- Basin Bloom + Ridge Midge = mutualism.

Change challenge:
- Bluecrest decline should first affect Ridgebacks and Valley Hoppers; later predator abundance or feeding pressure may change.

- Canopy Glider + Fruit Nibbler for Glowfruit fruit.

Core network:

For Mimsy, if you want the printed Food Web V1 to include every feeding resource as a separate node, have students add **Surface Insects** as a small consumer/prey group beside the provided organism records. This is also a useful reminder that ecological models are updated as new evidence appears.

## Common misconceptions to watch for
### “The arrow means who eats whom.”
Redirect to: **Which direction is the energy moving?**

### “Decomposers recycle energy.”
Matter/nutrients can cycle; energy does not return to the producer in a closed loop.

### “A food web tells exactly what will happen.”
A food web supports predictions, not certainties. Population size, behaviour, season, resource abundance, and many abiotic factors affect actual outcomes.

### “Competition means organisms fight.”
Competition can occur simply because organisms use the same limited resource. Direct fighting is not required.

### “Symbiosis means both species benefit.”
Mutualism is one form of symbiosis. Commensalism and parasitism are also included in this operation.

### “Decomposers only matter after everything dies.”
Waste and dead material are continuously entering decomposition pathways.

## Continental Archive submission
One student submits for the team through the JCEC Submission Portal.

Use:
- **Submission Type:** Continental Archive
- **Mission Phase:** 01 First Contact
- **Operation / Task:** Ecosystem Network — Food Web V1
- **Continent:** assigned continent
- **Team ID:** teacher-assigned phase + continent + team number, e.g. `P1-GY-01`
- **Team Members:** everyone represented by the submission

Written archive summary should include:
1. the focal species’ role;
2. why the ecosystem is a web rather than one chain;
3. at least three specific ecological connections;
4. an explanation of energy flow;
5. one direct and one indirect predicted effect from the change challenge;
6. one uncertainty or missing relationship.

## Individual accountability
After the team archive is complete, each student reflects on:
- two different connections for one organism;
- food-web arrow direction using the word energy;
- one indirect-effect prediction and its evidence;
- one relationship they would want JCEC to measure next.

Keep these in the Pathfinder Log unless individual submission is needed for assessment.

## Assessment opportunities
Formative evidence can include:
- accurate producer/consumer/decomposer classifications;
- correct food-web arrow direction;
- inclusion of decomposers;
- ability to distinguish food-web links from competition/symbiosis;
- evidence-supported direct and indirect predictions;
- recognition of model limitations and uncertainty;
- quality of Food Web V1 organization;
- strength of the written archive explanation.

Suggested recurring rubric dimensions:
- Science Understanding
- Evidence
- Reasoning
- Communication / Decision-Making

## Next narrative step
Operation 04 answers: **How are organisms connected, and how could a change spread through the ecosystem?**

Operation 05 will ask: **What do actual population data show, and which explanation best fits the evidence?**

Do not reveal later succession, invasive-species, contamination, or final settlement twists during Operation 04.
