# Grade 7 Math Unit 4 internal build specification

## Approved identity and sequence

**Unit 4 — Circles, Area, and Circle Graphs** follows the approved course map and the *Math Makes Sense 7* sequence: 4.1 Investigating Circles; 4.2 Circumference of a Circle; 4.3 Area of a Parallelogram; 4.4 Area of a Triangle; 4.5 Area of a Circle; 4.6 Interpreting Circle Graphs; 4.7 Drawing Circle Graphs. The textbook heading “Circles and Area” is shorter, but its same unit contains Lessons 4.6–4.7. The approved map intentionally names that content explicitly.

## Outcomes and achievement indicators

- **SS1:** describe radius–diameter–circumference relationships; show diameter is twice radius; show circumference is about three diameters; explain π = C/d ≈ 3.14; show central angles total 360°; construct a circle from a radius or diameter, with and without a compass; solve contextual radius, diameter, and circumference problems.
- **SS2:** connect rectangle area to triangle and parallelogram area; generalize `A = bh/2` and `A = bh`; estimate circle area without a formula; apply `A = πr²`; solve contextual area problems involving triangles, parallelograms, and circles.
- **SP3:** identify circle-graph attributes (title, labels/legend, 360° total, 100% total); create and label a circle graph with and without technology; compare circle graphs; translate sector percentages to quantities; interpret circle graphs to solve problems.

## Progression and pacing

Lessons move from circle language and construction to circumference, then area formulas built by rearrangement, then circle graphs using the already-secure central-angle and percent ideas. Each lesson uses two 35–40 minute sessions. Core session 1: readiness, Big Ideas, two essential Understand cards, one example, Explore Predict/Build, practice 1–2. Core session 2: one targeted Understand card, one example, Explore Verify, practice 3–4, five-question Check Yourself, and exit ticket. Practice 5–8, Explore Repair/Create, additional examples, and Apply are consolidation or extension.

## Representations and interactions

Concrete/visual → symbolic → contextual development uses virtual compass/chords, string-and-ruler circumference measurement, cut-and-slide parallelograms, duplicate-and-rotate triangles, sector rearrangement, proportional circle sectors, and a protractor-style circle-graph studio. Every Explore has a checkable prediction, operable controls, visible model, matching transcript, independently checked checkpoint, recorded evidence, equivalent valid states, stale-state invalidation, a supplied misconception repair, and a constrained creation target. Apply tasks independently validate required values, model state, unit, comparison/reasonableness result, and decision; prose is retained only as review evidence.

## Assessment and repair allocation

Every lesson has one prerequisite warm-up, eight supported-practice items, a separate 30-item Check Yourself bank, and a three-item exit ticket. Exact repair destinations use the matching `#understand-1` through `#understand-4` heading. The review contains seven model stations, 20 separate mixed-review items, at least ten misconception repairs, a separate 40-item Unit Check, and balanced ten-question attempts with every lesson represented. Recommendations are prioritized by percentage, missed count, then prerequisite order.

## Accessibility and responsive requirements

All essential models include readable visible labels and equivalent text transcripts. Interactions support keyboard operation, visible focus, status announcements, focus recovery, non-colour feedback, 44 px targets, reduced motion, and text enlargement. At tablet, 390 px, 375 px, and 320 px, controls stack, long mathematics wraps, model panels remain usable without page-level horizontal scrolling, and tables/model canvases use contained scrolling only when necessary.
