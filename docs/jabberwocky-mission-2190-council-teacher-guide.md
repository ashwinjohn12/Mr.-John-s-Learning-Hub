# Mission 2190 Council — Teacher Guide

## Purpose
The Mission 2190 Council is the final **3-class year-long transfer task** for Jabberwocky: Mission 2190. It is not a sixth science unit and not a vocabulary exam.

Students keep their **Phase 5 teams and continents** and become **JCEC Council Delegations**. They do not reopen old Team Records.

## Student evidence source
Each delegation receives exactly five concise Evidence Vault cards:

1. **ECOSYSTEM EVIDENCE + ECOLOGICAL SAFEGUARD** — Phase 1
2. **PLANT RESOURCE EVIDENCE + SUSTAINABILITY RULE** — Phase 2
3. **THERMAL EVIDENCE + SURVIVAL / ENERGY RULE** — Phase 3
4. **STRUCTURAL EVIDENCE + SAFETY / SITE RULE** — Phase 4
5. **GEOLOGICAL EVIDENCE + LIMIT / UNCERTAINTY / PROTECTION RULE** — Phase 5

Cards contain evidence and constraints only. They do **not** contain the final Council recommendation.

## Final question
**How and where, if anywhere, should humans establish a long-term presence on Jabberwocky—and what must remain protected?**

## Recording mode
The Council website is the shared **evidence, prompt and candidate-selection surface**. It does **not** save written Council responses.

Students should keep their official Five-Lens Review, two-option comparison, Council Directive and individual reflection on a printed sheet, ordinary paper or another teacher-approved document. Candidate buttons on the website are temporary and reset after a page refresh.

## Class 1 — Open the Evidence Vault
**BEFORE CLASS:** Confirm Phase 5 teams/continents and verify the saved Phase 5 posting loads the correct Evidence Vault. Set the recording method before students begin.

**MATERIALS:** Evidence Vault on screen or print, Five-Lens Council Review recording sheet or ordinary paper, pencils. Chromebooks/projector are optional for viewing and candidate selection.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Read all five cards; identify what each lens supports, limits, protects and leaves uncertain; record the Five-Lens Council Review on paper; identify two plausible Council outcomes.

**KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS:** Evidence from five science units must be combined; evidence is not itself a recommendation.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Students may not choose an outcome first and search for support afterward. No continent has a predetermined answer. The website does not save written Council responses.

**COLLECT / ASSESS:** Five-Lens Council Review + two plausible candidates.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Require one strong SUPPORTS/LIMITS/PROTECTS/UNCERTAIN entry per lens and two candidate outcomes.

## Class 2 — The Council Deliberates
**BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare outcome cards and the two-option comparison board.

**MATERIALS:** Five-Lens Review, website outcome cards, comparison board on paper or ordinary paper, pencils. Chromebooks/projector are optional for viewing and temporary candidate selection.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Compare at least two outcomes; identify support, limit, safeguard and uncertainty for each; record a draft directive.

**KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS:** Scientific decisions include trade-offs, safeguards and uncertainty.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Environmental protection matters as much as human convenience. Delaying expansion for more evidence is a legitimate outcome. Candidate buttons are temporary and may reset after a refresh; the written comparison is the official record.

**COLLECT / ASSESS:** Two-option comparison + draft directive.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Compare exactly two options and require one explicit trade-off, safeguard and uncertainty.

## Class 3 — Issue the Mission 2190 Directive
**BEFORE CLASS:** Prepare one Council Directive per team and one reflection prompt per student. The Council page **Print / Save Directive** button provides the blank final sheet with the saved continent filled in; ordinary paper is also acceptable.

**MATERIALS:** Evidence Vault, completed review, Council Directive or ordinary paper, pencils. Chromebook/projector is optional as a reference surface.

**STUDENTS SEE / DO:** Finalize human-presence level, where/scale, five evidence lenses, three safeguards, protection statement, uncertainty, next investigation, 4–6 sentence Council message and individual reflection.

**KEY SCIENCE / SYNTHESIS:** A scientific recommendation should be evidence-based, revisable and explicit about uncertainty.

**TEACHER EMPHASIS:** Assess evidence integration and reasoning rather than polish or vocabulary recall. Written work belongs on the printed sheet, ordinary paper or another teacher-approved document—not in the website itself.

**COLLECT / ASSESS:** **Mission 2190 Council Directive + individual reflection**.

**IF TIME RUNS OUT:** Protect the final directive, five science lenses, three safeguards, one uncertainty and individual reflection. Cut presentations first.

## Possible Council outcomes
- **LONG-TERM PRESENCE WITH STRONG SAFEGUARDS**
- **LIMITED / SEASONAL PRESENCE**
- **RESEARCH-ONLY PRESENCE**
- **PROTECTED AREA / RESTRICTED HUMAN ACTIVITY**
- **DELAY LONG-TERM EXPANSION / COLLECT MORE EVIDENCE**

Mixed directives are allowed when supported by evidence, such as limited presence plus a protected area.

## Final reasoning scaffold
**EVIDENCE → IMPLICATION → SAFEGUARD → UNCERTAINTY → DIRECTIVE**

## Final Team Record
The **MISSION 2190 COUNCIL DIRECTIVE** contains:
- continent
- human-presence directive
- where / scale
- ecosystem evidence
- plant resource evidence
- thermal evidence
- structural evidence
- geological evidence
- 3 non-negotiable safeguards
- what must remain protected
- one important uncertainty
- what JCEC should investigate next
- final Council message (4–6 sentences)

## Individual reflection
**Which piece of evidence most changed your thinking about human presence on Jabberwocky, and what new evidence could make you change your decision again?**

## Assessment role
Treat the Council as the year-long transfer task. Suggested criteria:
1. Evidence integration
2. Reasoning / trade-offs
3. Safeguards + uncertainty
4. Final communication

Do not add points for art, elaborate presentation or memorized wording from old assignments.

## Low-material implementation
Preferred: projected/printed Evidence Vault + Council Review + Council Directive + pencils.

Chromebooks/projector are useful for reading evidence, prompts and temporary candidate selection, but **not** for storing written Council responses.

**No-purchase fallback:** project the five cards and use ordinary paper for the review, comparison, directive and reflection.

No lab equipment, model, kit or new purchase is needed.

## Two-class compressed fallback
**Class 1:** Evidence Vault + Five-Lens Review + compare two outcomes + choose draft directive.

**Class 2:** Complete Council Directive + individual reflection + story closure.

Cut gallery walks and presentations before cutting evidence review, safeguards, uncertainty or reflection.

## Guardrails
- Keep Phase 5 teams and continents.
- Do not reopen old Team Records.
- Do not invent new Jabberwocky hazards, resources, energy systems, structures, fossils, faults or ecosystem conditions.
- Do not predetermine a correct Council status by continent.
- Allow uncertainty to limit or delay action.
- Make safeguards part of the final decision.
- Treat website candidate selections as temporary; the written comparison is the official record.
- End Mission 2190 after the Council Directive and reflection.
